\title{Automated End-to-End 3-Axis CNC Machining: Bi-Level Task-and-Motion Planning, Differentiable Cutting Simulation, and Trajectory-Policy Co-Optimization}

\author{Author Names Omitted for Anonymous Review}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Advances in fused-deposition 3D printing have made additive fabrication accessible to non-experts: a user drops a digital model into slicer software, which automatically plans and executes trajectories in $\mathbb{R}^4$ $(x, y, z, \text{extrude})$. That accessibility has not transferred to 3-axis Computer Numerical Control (CNC) milling. Although 3-axis CNC trajectories inhabit a superficially similar continuous action space $\mathbb{R}^4$ $(x, y, z, \text{spindle/cutting speed})$, subtractive machining requires first solving a combinatorial upstream task-planning problem—selecting raw stock alloy and dimensions, $SO(3)$ setup orientation and multi-setup workholding, multi-class cutting/drilling/chamfering/threading tool cribs, feature-bound toolpath topologies, and narrow physically admissible bands of feeds and speeds—followed by long-horizon trajectory optimization through discontinuous Boolean material removal. Today, this hierarchy demands hours of manual configuration by expert CNC programmers. We challenge this paradigm with \textbf{DiffCAM}, an MIT-licensed, end-to-end automated 3-axis CNC machining framework that unifies (1) bi-level task-and-motion planning (TAMP) over arbitrary ISO~10303-21 \texttt{.step} B-Rep and \texttt{.stl} CAD solids, 6-sided $SO(3)$ setup scoring with automated \texttt{Op1 (G54)} / \texttt{Op2 (G55)} soft-jaw flipping, a 673-record US/Metric clean-room tool catalog with customizable shop tool cribs, and a 6-constraint \texttt{feeds\_and\_speeds} physics solver; (2) classical clean-room CAM trajectory initialization across datum facing, Euclidean-EDT 2.5D roughing/rest-finishing, trochoidal clearing, inverse-offset 3D ball-endmill scallop surfacing, helical boring, \texttt{G83} peck drilling, chamfering, and climb thread milling; and (3) dual-mode differentiable cutting simulation combining 3D swept-cylinder signed distance fields (SDFs) under Straight-Through Estimator (STE) continuation with chronological 2.5D cutter-workpiece engagement (CWE) chip-thinning mechanics and exact \texttt{G187} junction-blending gradients. Across 16 single-tool benchmark tasks ($B = 64$ simulator calls), smooth-to-hard STE continuation cuts cycle time by $\mathbf{42.0 \pm 13.5\%}$ at $100\%$ Boolean feasibility ($4\times$ evolutionary search), while first-order \texttt{DiffPolicy-SHAC} doubles the sample efficiency of zero-order \texttt{Hard-PPO} ($\mathbf{37.0\%}$ vs.\ $\mathbf{18.5\%}$). Under matched-speed and optimal-chipload CWE evaluation (\textbf{Exp5}--\textbf{Exp5b}), \texttt{DiffCAM-Chipload} maintains $\mathbf{97.7\%}$ of engaged cutting inside the optimal undeformed chip-thickness band ($h_{\text{ex}}$ RMSE $0.0053\text{ mm}$), and inference-time trajectory overfitting from zero-shot \texttt{SHAC} ($3.5\text{ ms}$) or CAM seeds converges in $0.48\text{--}1.04\text{ s}$. Finally, across 4 multi-alloy \texttt{.stl} parts (\textbf{Exp6}) and all 10 arbitrary ISO~10303-21 \texttt{.step} parts of the \texttt{TITAN-1M--10M} aerospace benchmark (\textbf{Exp7}, spanning 6--11 tools/part, \texttt{G54+G55} flips, 3D B-spline domes/airfoils, and internal threads), DiffCAM achieves $\mathbf{100\%}$ safety pass ($\mathbf{0.000\text{ mm}^3}$ gouge), $\mathbf{36.3\%}$ fair matched-speed savings, and $\mathbf{41.6\%}$ optimal-chipload cycle-time reduction across $60/60$ compiled 6-dialect NC programs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Roboticists in the past have often dismissed the robotic challenges of manufacturing because the factory domain appears relatively well-defined and structured rather than addressing open-ended, general-purpose manipulation tasks. Yet creating physical objects from digital designs via machine tools has historically required deep artistic and technical expertise. Advances in desktop 3D printing have demonstrated that technical expertise in manufacturing processes is not inherently required to manifest digital objects in the physical world: a user no longer needs to understand toolpaths, kinematics, or robotics, and can instead simply drop a digital object into a 3D printer's slicer software, which automatically finds and executes the toolpaths required to extrude plastic in the shape of the part.

However, the plug-and-play accessibility of 3D printing software has not transferred to more complex manufacturing processes, such as 3-axis CNC milling machines. At the continuous motion level, the instantaneous action space for a typical fused-deposition modeling (FDM) 3D printer is $\mathbb{R}^4$ $(x, y, z, \text{extrude})$, and the action space for 3-axis CNC machining trajectories is superficially similar—$\mathbb{R}^4$ $(x, y, z, \text{spindle/cutting speed})$. Relative to 3D printing, CNC machining allows the fast and repeatable creation of strong, precision parts out of both common and exotic structural alloys. However, because machining is a \emph{subtractive} process—in which a rotating cutter permanently removes solid metal and every pass irreversibly alters downstream cutter engagement~\cite{altintas2012manufacturing,suh2008cnc}—much more upstream planning is required prior to choosing a continuous trajectory:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Stock, Orientation, and Multi-Setup Workholding:} One must first select a piece of raw material, called \emph{stock} (including material alloy and billet dimensions), choose the 3D orientation of the stock inside the CNC work envelope, and determine how it is rigidly clamped down against cutting forces, called \emph{workholding} (such as parallel vise jaws or custom soft jaws). Whenever underside pockets, counterbores, or blind holes are occluded from above, the part must also be flipped across multiple work offsets (\texttt{G54}, \texttt{G55}) without colliding with the vise jaws.
  \item \textbf{Multi-Class Tool Cribs and Operation Topologies:} Whereas 3D printers typically operate with a single nozzle size and one filament corresponding to a single operation (extruding plastic), cost- and time-efficiently removing metal from stock requires a \emph{library of tools} spanning distinct classes of operations: \emph{cutting tools} (face, flat, bull-nose, ball, and chamfer end mills), \emph{hole-making tools} (spot drills, twist drills, countersinks, reamers), \emph{threading tools} (taps, thread mills), and \emph{spindle touch probes}. Each tool corresponds to specialized parameterized \emph{toolpaths}: drills plunge in a single motion or execute incremental pecks and retract to evacuate chips before plunging deeper (\texttt{G83}); cutting tools slice a thin datum layer off the top of the stock via a \emph{facing} path, hog out bulk pockets rapidly via \emph{roughing} or trochoidal clearing~\cite{held1991pocket,rauch2009trochoidal}, rest-mill tight corners left behind by larger cutters, or finish sculptured 3D domes and airfoils via ball-endmill scallop passes~\cite{choi1998sculptured}. Moreover, any real shop only stocks a finite subset of tools (its \emph{tool crib}), requiring automatic fallback strategies such as helical bore interpolation when an exact drill size is unavailable.
  \item \textbf{Narrow Admissible Feeds and Speeds:} Tools and toolpaths admit only a narrow physical band of configurations—specifically the rotational speed of the tool relative to linear motion against the workpiece, known as \emph{feeds and speeds}~\cite{martellotti1941milling,altintas2012manufacturing}. Feeding below minimum chip thickness causes rubbing and work-hardening; feeding too aggressively exceeds spindle power, core shear torque, or cantilever deflection limits and fractures the cutter.
\end{enumerate}

The combination of stock material, workholding, tool selection, toolpath choice, and feeds and speeds creates a massive hierarchical planning space that must be solved before finding an optimal trajectory under the conditions of the plan. Today, people solve the complexity of CNC machining in a predominantly manual way: expert CNC programmers spend many hours in visual CAM tools manually configuring stock, workholding, US or metric tool-library assemblies, feature-associated parameterized toolpaths, and handbook- or calculator-derived feeds and speeds.

We challenge this paradigm and propose that 3-axis CNC machining should be end-to-end solvable in an automated way—at a quality level that currently requires hours of expert programmer time—by coupling task-level setup and multi-tool planning prior to trajectory optimization with classical CAM trajectory initialization, physics-grounded feeds and speeds, and differentiable cutting simulation. Achieving this requires resolving three core algorithmic challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Bi-Level Coupling Between Discrete Tool Cribs and Persistent Stock Geometry:} Upstream tool choices govern both automatic-tool-changer (ATC) swap overhead and the exact evolving in-process workpiece (IPW) encountered by every downstream rest-milling, 3D surfacing, and hole-making tool across single or dual setups.
  \item \textbf{Discontinuous Boolean Subtraction, Radial Chip Thinning, and Corner Blending:} Exact Boolean subtraction $\phi_{t+1} = \max(\phi_t, -\psi_{\text{tool}})$~\cite{vanhook1986dexel,shapiro2007rfunctions} has zero gradients in air and kinks on cut boundaries, naive smooth surrogates suffer volumetric drift~\cite{suh2022differentiable,metz2021gradients}, relative waypoint deltas accumulate $O(T)$ drift, and naive feed boosting along curved polylines triggers severe CNC junction deceleration penalties (\texttt{G64}/\texttt{G187})~\cite{erkorkmaz2001highspeed,kramer2000rs274ngc} or distorts actual undeformed chip thickness $h_{\text{ex}}$ under partial radial immersion~\cite{martellotti1941milling}.
  \item \textbf{Amortized RL Generalization vs.\ Per-Part Test-Time Optimality:} Offline-trained RL policies infer in milliseconds on unseen parts but suffer finite-capacity approximation error, motivating fast test-time trajectory overfitting through analytical simulator gradients.
\end{enumerate}

To address this full stack, we present \textbf{DiffCAM} (Figs.~\ref{fig:hero} and~\ref{fig:system_arch}). Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Arbitrary STEP/STL Multi-Setup Bi-Level TAMP \& Configurable Tool Cribs:} We build an MIT clean-room catalog of $673$ US/Metric tools and holders (\texttt{StandardToolLibrary}), a 6-constraint \texttt{feeds\_and\_speeds} physics engine, an ISO~10303-21 \texttt{.step} B-Rep + \texttt{.stl} feature recognizer (\texttt{cam.step\_brep}), and a bi-level TAMP planner (\texttt{cam.tamp\_planner}) supporting 6-sided $SO(3)$ setup orientation, \texttt{Op1 (G54)} + \texttt{Op2 (G55)} soft-jaw flipping, crib-constrained fallback synthesis, and persistent-IPW propagation across 10 operation classes.
  \item \textbf{Dual-Mode Differentiable Cutting Simulation with Exact Junction-Blending Gradients:} We formulate (a) a GPU Taichi~\cite{hu2020difftaichi} 3D swept-SDF simulator with Straight-Through Estimator (STE)~\cite{bengio2013estimating} stiffness continuation ($k: 25 \to 90$), and (b) a chronological 2.5D dexel CWE optimizer modeling Martellotti radial chip thinning, swept circular-endcap immersion, full-cutter-radius-verified \texttt{G0} air shortcutting, and closed-form \texttt{G187} junction-velocity gradients $\partial T_{\text{cycle}}/\partial v_k$.
  \item \textbf{Controlled RL Benchmark and Sub-Second Inference-Time Trajectory Overfitting:} Across 16 tasks and 8 methods under strict simulator-call parity, we compare first-order (\texttt{STE-Continuation-Adam}, \texttt{DiffPolicy-SHAC}~\cite{xu2022accelerated}) vs.\ zero-order (\texttt{Hard-PPO}~\cite{schulman2017proximal}, \texttt{CEM}~\cite{rubinstein2004cem}, \texttt{CMA-ES}~\cite{hansen2001cmaes}) methods, isolate fair matched-speed vs.\ optimal-chipload gains (\textbf{Exp5}), and demonstrate sub-second inference-time trajectory overfitting (\textbf{Exp5b}).
  \item \textbf{End-to-End Verification on Arbitrary Aerospace STEP Solids and Live 3D Tool-Library WebGL:} Beyond $48/48$ single-tool 6-dialect and \texttt{CAMotics v1.2.0}~\cite{coffland2021camotics} watertight STL audits (\textbf{Exp4}) and 4 multi-alloy \texttt{.stl} parts (\textbf{Exp6}), DiffCAM machines all $10/10$ ISO~10303-21 \texttt{.step} solids of the \texttt{TITAN-1M--10M} benchmark (\textbf{Exp7}) with $0.000\text{ mm}^3$ gouge and $41.6\%$ mean cycle-time savings, paired with an interactive CAD-upload, 3D tool-assembly, and 6-dialect G-code WebGL simulator.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) and CAD Feature Recognition.}
Integrated TAMP couples discrete symbolic sequencing—such as grasp, mode, or tool selection—with continuous geometric feasibility and motion optimization~\cite{kaelbling2011hpn,garrett2021tamp}. While widely studied in robotic manipulation and additive extrusion, subtractive CNC process planning has traditionally treated STEP B-Rep feature recognition~\cite{iso10303step,suh2008cnc} separately from downstream toolpath generation. DiffCAM formulates 3-axis machining as a bi-level persistent-IPW TAMP problem: upstream $SO(3)$ setup orientation, multi-setup \texttt{G54}/\texttt{G55} partitioning, and crib-constrained DAG tool selection minimize ATC-penalized machining time while propagating exact carved dexel stock across tools so downstream rest-finishing, 3D surfacing, and hole-making trajectories adapt to actual remaining metal.

\textbf{Geometric CAM, Sculptured Surfacing, and Engagement Physics.}
Classical 2.5D pocketing uses 2D medial-axis/distance offsets~\cite{held1991pocket}, which suffer sharp radial immersion spikes in concave corners, motivating trochoidal clearing~\cite{rauch2009trochoidal} and Z-buffer dexel~\cite{vanhook1986dexel}, R-function~\cite{shapiro2007rfunctions}, or octree~\cite{coffland2021camotics} verification. Freeform 3D surfaces require inverse-offset cutter-location ($z_{\text{CL}}$) envelopes to prevent ball-endmill gouging~\cite{choi1998sculptured}. Physically, when radial immersion $a_e < D/2$, Martellotti's kinematics~\cite{martellotti1941milling,altintas2012manufacturing} dictates that maximum undeformed chip thickness $h_{\text{ex}}$ thins below feed per tooth $f_z$, requiring feed compensation bounded by spindle power and cantilever beam deflection. Whereas prior feedrate schedulers modulate speed along a \emph{frozen} toolpath~\cite{erkorkmaz2001highspeed}, DiffCAM jointly optimizes continuous XY waypoints, stock-verified air shortcuts, and per-station feeds through differentiable CWE and corner-blending kinematics~\cite{kramer2000rs274ngc}.

\textbf{Differentiable Simulation, RL, and Test-Time Optimization.}
Differentiable simulators~\cite{hu2020difftaichi,freeman2021brax} enable low-variance first-order trajectory optimization and short-horizon analytic actor-critic (\texttt{SHAC}) learning~\cite{xu2022accelerated}, though discontinuous contact or Boolean clipping can introduce gradient bias or variance~\cite{suh2022differentiable,metz2021gradients} relative to zero-order \texttt{PPO}~\cite{schulman2017proximal,schulman2016gae}, \texttt{CEM}~\cite{rubinstein2004cem}, and \texttt{CMA-ES}~\cite{hansen2001cmaes}. Crucially, we show that analytical simulator gradients unlock sub-second \emph{inference-time trajectory overfitting} from either an amortized RL warm-start or a clean-room CAM seed—an operating regime where test-time model-free RL fails due to $\mathcal{O}(D/\sigma^2)$ gradient variance.

\section{System Architecture and Bi-Level Task-and-Motion Planning}
\label{sec:formulation}

Figure~\ref{fig:system_arch} illustrates the five stages of DiffCAM. To ensure complete conceptual reproducibility, Sections~\ref{sec:cad_setup}--\ref{sec:cam_seeds} specify every representation and algorithmic rule in the upstream TAMP and CAM-initialization pipeline, followed in Section~\ref{sec:diffsim_methods} by our dual-mode differentiable simulators, RL policies, and post-processors.

\subsection{CAD Ingestion, Trimmed B-Rep Tessellation, and 6-Sided $SO(3)$ Setup Synthesis}
\label{sec:cad_setup}
\textbf{ISO~10303-21 STEP B-Rep and STL Parsing (\texttt{cam.step\_brep}):}
DiffCAM accepts either watertight binary/ASCII \texttt{.stl} triangle meshes or native ISO~10303-21 (\texttt{AP203}/\texttt{AP214}) \texttt{.step} boundary-representation solids~\cite{iso10303step}. Our clean-room STEP parser tokenizes the \texttt{DATA} entity graph, resolves \texttt{LENGTH\_\allowbreak MEASURE\_\allowbreak WITH\_\allowbreak UNIT} (\texttt{INCH} $\times 25.4 \to \text{mm}$ vs.\ \texttt{MILLI}/\texttt{METRE}), and extracts every \texttt{ADVANCED\_\allowbreak FACE} with its analytical or freeform carrier surface (\texttt{PLANE}, \texttt{CYLINDRICAL\_\allowbreak SURFACE}, \texttt{CONICAL\_\allowbreak SURFACE}, \texttt{TOROIDAL\_\allowbreak SURFACE}, \texttt{SPHERICAL\_\allowbreak SURFACE}, and rational/non-rational \texttt{B\_\allowbreak SPLINE\_\allowbreak SURFACE\_\allowbreak WITH\_\allowbreak KNOTS}) together with oriented outer and inner \texttt{EDGE\_\allowbreak LOOP} trim curves (\texttt{LINE}, \texttt{CIRCLE}, \texttt{ELLIPSE}, \texttt{B\_\allowbreak SPLINE\_\allowbreak CURVE}). Each face is projected into its local 2D $(u,v)$ parameter domain (handling $2\pi$ periodic seam unwrapping on cylinders, cones, and tori), inner hole loops are connected to the outer boundary via minimum-distance bridge cuts, and the resulting simple polygon is ear-clipped and lifted onto an interior $(u,v)$ Steiner grid so curved surfaces are tessellated to chordal tolerance $\le 0.08\text{ mm}$ while retaining exact analytical face-type, axis, radius, and semi-angle metadata on every triangle.

\textbf{6-Cardinal $SO(3)$ Setup Orientation Selection:}
Given the tessellated solid $\mathcal{M}_0$, the planner evaluates the six cardinal axis-aligned rotations $\mathcal{R}_6 \subset SO(3)$ mapping $\{\pm X, \pm Y, \pm Z\} \mapsto +Z$ (with longest horizontal dimension aligned along machine $+X$, i.e., parallel to the vise jaws). For each candidate $\mathbf{R} \in \mathcal{R}_6$, we rasterize upper and lower heightfields at pitch $\Delta x = 0.25\text{ mm}$ and score
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    S_{\text{setup}}(\mathbf{R}) =\;& w_{\text{vis}} A_{+Z}(\mathbf{R}) + w_{\text{feat}} N_{+Z}^{\text{feat}}(\mathbf{R}) \\
    &+ w_{\text{clamp}} A_{\text{clamp}}(\mathbf{R}) - w_{\text{ovh}} A_{\text{undercut}}(\mathbf{R}) \\
    &- w_H L_z(\mathbf{R}),
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:setup_score}
\end{equation}
where $A_{+Z}$ is upward-visible machined pocket/step/sculptured surface area, $N_{+Z}^{\text{feat}}$ counts upward-accessible hole/bore axes, $A_{\text{clamp}}$ measures parallel vertical side-wall datum contact along $\pm Y$ for rigid vise gripping, $A_{\text{undercut}}$ penalizes occluded downward-facing cavities requiring a flip, and $L_z(\mathbf{R})$ penalizes tall Z-height that demands long, deflection-prone tool stickout. Selecting $\mathbf{R}^\star = \arg\max_{\mathbf{R} \in \mathcal{R}_6} S_{\text{setup}}(\mathbf{R})$ automatically reorients parts authored on side planes (e.g., mapping $+Y \mapsto +Z$ on \texttt{Titan-M2, M3, M5, M6, M9, M10}) without user intervention.

\textbf{Stock Billet, Parallel Vise Jaws, and Multi-Setup (\texttt{G54}/\texttt{G55}) Feature Partition:}
In frame $\mathbf{R}^\star$, the part's top datum is placed at $z = -\delta_{\text{top}}$ ($\delta_{\text{top}} \in [1.0, 1.5]\text{ mm}$) inside a rectangular stock billet $[x_{\min}, x_{\max}] \times [y_{\min}, y_{\max}] \times [-L_z, 0]$, where $L_z = H_{\text{part}} + \delta_{\text{top}} + \delta_{\text{grip}}$ includes a sacrificial bottom carrier-hat clamping grip $\delta_{\text{grip}} \in [2.0, 3.5]\text{ mm}$ held between forbidden parallel vise jaws $\mathcal{V}_{-Y}, \mathcal{V}_{+Y}$ spanning $z \in [-L_z, z_{\text{jaw}}]$. All \texttt{Op1 (G54)} cutting depths are clamped to $z \ge z_{\text{jaw}} + 0.35\text{ mm}$ to guarantee jaw clearance.

Coaxial cylindrical and conical face groups (or circular STL wall loops) are assembled into compound hole stacks recording bore diameter $D_h$, $[z_{\text{top}}, z_{\text{bot}}]$, counterbore $(D_{\text{cb}}, z_{\text{cb}})$, countersink $(D_{\text{cs}}, \alpha_{\text{cs}} \in \{82^\circ, 90^\circ\})$, $45^\circ$ edge-chamfer chains, and internal ANSI Unified / ISO metric threads detected when bore diameter matches standard tap-drill minor diameter $D_{\text{minor}} \approx D_{\text{major}} - 1.0825\,P$ (e.g., \texttt{\#10-24}, \texttt{1/4-20}, \texttt{M6$\times$1}). Freeform B-spline faces with tilted normals ($0.15 < |n_z| < 0.985$) are segmented into connected 3D sculptured regions. Crucially, to distinguish true through-holes from blind underside features, the planner casts $+Z$ rays from every feature's interior: any cavity, counterbore, or blind hole occluded from $+Z$ by overlying solid part roof ($\exists (x, y, z') \in \mathcal{M}$ with $z' > z_{\text{feat}} + \varepsilon$), or any part requiring bottom carrier-hat removal to expose underside islands/profiles, automatically triggers a second setup (\textbf{\texttt{Op2}}, work offset \texttt{G55}) under the $180^\circ$ X-axis soft-jaw flip
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{F}(x, y, z) = (x,\; -y,\; -L_z - z),
  \label{eq:op2_flip}
\end{equation}
inserted after a posted machine stop (\texttt{M00}) so the operator flips the Op1-machined profile into custom soft jaws.

\subsection{Bi-Level TAMP: Crib-Constrained DAG Search and Persistent-IPW Coupling}
\label{sec:tamp}
How are discrete task planning and continuous motion optimization coupled? DiffCAM solves a bi-level hierarchical program over our 100\% clean-room \texttt{StandardToolLibrary} $\mathcal{T}$ ($673$ total records: $601$ US imperial and metric face mills, flat/bull/ball end mills, chamfer/countersink cutters, spot/twist drills, reamers, taps, single-/multi-form thread mills, and \texttt{T99} touch probes, plus $72$ BT30/CAT40/HSK/ISO20 holders across $15$ library groups and $32$ entity types):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Shop Tool Crib Filtering and Geometric/Reach Admissibility:} Given a user-selected shop crib $\mathcal{C} \subseteq \mathcal{T}$ (e.g., US Imperial Crib, Metric Shop Crib, or an explicit subset of in-magazine tools), a tool $\tau \in \mathcal{C}$ is admissible for feature $f$ iff: (a) its cutting diameter satisfies local feature clearance ($D \le \min(2 r_{\min}(f), w_{\min}(f) - \delta_c)$ for rest-finishing; $0.50 D_{\text{bore}} \le D \le 0.88 D_{\text{bore}}$ for helical bores; $D \le 0.82 D_{\text{minor}}$ with matching pitch $P$ for thread mills), and (b) assembly stickout clears feature depth below stock top without holder interference ($L_{\text{stick}}(\tau) \ge \Delta z(f) + \delta_{\text{hld}}$).
  \item \textbf{Crib-Constrained Fallback Synthesis:} When an exact twist drill for diameter $D_h$ is absent from $\mathcal{C}$, TAMP automatically synthesizes either (i) helical bore interpolation using an admissible flat endmill ($D \le 0.85 D_h$) for medium/large bores, or (ii) pilot drilling with the largest admissible drill $D_{\text{pilot}} < D_h$ in $\mathcal{C}$ (logging explicit setup advisories if a micro-thread cutter such as \texttt{\#2-56} is excluded from the active crib).
  \item \textbf{10-Stage Technological Precedence DAG:} Within each setup ($s \in \{\text{G54}, \text{G55}\}$), operations follow a strict subtractive precedence DAG $\mathcal{G}_{\text{prec}}$ so datum registration and bulk stock removal precede precision finishing and hole/thread operations: (1) \texttt{T99} WIPS datum probing $\prec$ (2) top/carrier-hat datum facing $\prec$ (3) bulk 2.5D level-set roughing $\prec$ (4) persistent-IPW corner/wall rest-finishing $\prec$ (5) 3D ball-endmill scallop surfacing $\prec$ (6) helical counterbore/bore interpolation $\prec$ (7) $90^\circ$ spot drilling $\prec$ (8) \texttt{G83} peck twist drilling $\prec$ (9) countersinking and $45^\circ$ edge chamfering $\prec$ (10) internal climb thread milling.
  \item \textbf{ATC-Penalized Discrete Tool Selection:} Across candidate roughing/finishing/boring tool assignments in $\mathcal{C}$ satisfying $\mathcal{G}_{\text{prec}}$, the discrete planner minimizes estimated shop time including physical automatic-tool-changer swap latency $t_{\text{ATC}} = 6.0\text{ s}$:
  \begin{equation}
    J_{\text{task}}(\mathcal{S}) = \sum_{o \in \mathcal{S}} \frac{\widehat{V}_o}{\operatorname{MRR}(m_{\text{stock}}, \tau_o)} + \big(|\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S})| - 1\big)\,t_{\text{ATC}},
    \label{eq:tamp_cost}
  \end{equation}
  where $\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S})$ is the set of unique tools across sequence $\mathcal{S} = (o_1,\dots,o_O)$ and $\widehat{V}_o$ is the dexel volume assigned to operation $o$. Equation~\eqref{eq:tamp_cost} naturally consolidates operations onto shared cutters (e.g., reusing a single $1/4''$ flat endmill for both 2.5D rest-finishing and helical counterboring) whenever an extra tool change outweighs incremental MRR gains, and assigns persistent magazine numbers \texttt{T1..Tn} across \texttt{G54} and \texttt{G55}.
  \item \textbf{Chronological Persistent-IPW Propagation:} Rather than planning each operation against an unmachined block, the exact 2.5D dexel heightfield carved by optimized operation $o$ becomes the initial stock state for operation $o+1$:
  \begin{equation}
    z_{\text{IPW}}^{(o+1)}(x, y) \leftarrow z_{\text{final}}^{(o)}(x, y), \qquad o = 1, \dots, O - 1,
    \label{eq:ipw_update}
  \end{equation}
  with $z_{\text{final}}^{(O_1)}$ inverted via $\mathcal{F}$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:op2_flip}) at the \texttt{G54}$\to$\texttt{G55} boundary. Downstream rest-finishing, 3D ball-scallop, and hole/thread operations thus sense exact remaining stock, enter cuts just above the local IPW ceiling, and convert zero-removal traverses into collision-checked rapid bridges.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Clean-Room \texttt{feeds\_and\_speeds} Physics Engine}
\label{sec:feeds_speeds}
Every discrete TAMP evaluation and continuous CWE optimization queries our clean-room physics module (\texttt{cam.feeds\_and\_speeds}). Each alloy $m_{\text{stock}}$ specifies Brinell hardness, specific cutting energy $k_p$ ($\text{N/mm}^2$, e.g., $700$ for Al 6061-T6, $850$ for 7075-T6, $2100$ for 1018 Steel, $3400$ for Ti-6Al-4V), reference carbide surface speed $V_{c,0}$, reference chip load $f_{z,0}(D)$, and slotting derating $\gamma_{\text{slot}}$. Given cutter substrate ($E = 600\text{ GPa}$ carbide, $210\text{ GPa}$ HSS/cobalt), diameter $D$, flute count $z_f$, stickout $L_{\text{stick}}$, axial depth $a_p$, and radial immersion $a_e$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Spindle Speed and Thinning-Compensated Target Chip Load:} Spindle speed is clamped to machine bounds via $N_{\text{RPM}} = \operatorname{clip}\!\big(\frac{1000\,V_c}{\pi D}, N_{\min}, N_{\max}\big)$ with tooth-passing frequency $f_{\text{tooth}} = (N_{\text{RPM}}/60)\,z_f$ and cutting speed $v_c = \pi D N_{\text{RPM}} / 60000\text{ m/s}$. Target undeformed chip thickness is $h_{\text{opt}} = \gamma_{\text{op}}\,f_{z,0}(D)$ (scaled by $\gamma_{\text{slot}}$ when $a_e \ge 0.85 D$). Under partial radial immersion ($u = a_e/D < 0.5$) or shallow ball-endmill axial engagement ($a_p < R$), nominal feed per tooth boosts by $\min\!\big(2.2, \psi(u)^{-1}\big)$ (and $\min\!\big(2.0, \sqrt{R/\max(a_p,\varepsilon)}\big)$ for ball mills) so actual chip thickness matches $h_{\text{opt}}$.
  \item \textbf{Six Simultaneous Machine and Structural Clamps:} With volumetric removal rate $Q(f) = a_p a_e f$ ($\text{mm}^3/\text{s}$), cutting power $P(f) = 10^{-3} k_p Q(f)$ ($\text{W}$), tangential cutting force $F_c(f) = P(f) / v_c$ ($\text{N}$), and effective flute-core second moment of area $I = \frac{\pi}{64}(0.62 D)^4$, linear feedrate $f$ ($\text{mm/s}$) is simultaneously bounded by machine feed, spindle power, spindle torque $\tau_{\text{sp}}(N_{\text{RPM}})$, tool-core shear torque $\tau_{\text{core}}$, frame force $F_{\max}$, and cantilever tip deflection $\delta = \frac{F_c L_{\text{stick}}^3}{3 E I} \le \delta_{\max}$ ($0.012\text{ mm}$ finishing, $0.035\text{ mm}$ roughing):
  \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
      f_{\text{lim}} = \min\!\Big(&
        f_{\max},\;
        \frac{10^3 \eta P_{\max}}{k_p a_p a_e},\;
        \frac{10^3 \tau_{\text{sp}}\omega}{k_p a_p a_e},\;
        \frac{10^3 \tau_{\text{core}}\omega}{k_p a_p a_e}, \\
        &\frac{10^3 F_{\max} v_c}{k_p a_p a_e},\;
        \frac{3\cdot 10^3 E I \delta_{\max} v_c}{k_p a_p a_e L_{\text{stick}}^3}
      \Big),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:six_caps}
  \end{equation}
  where $\omega = 2\pi N_{\text{RPM}} / 60$. For twist drilling, axial thrust and torque determine feed per revolution $f_{\text{rev}}$ and automatically switch from continuous \texttt{G81} drilling to \texttt{G83} chip-breaking peck cycles ($\Delta z_{\text{peck}} = \kappa_{\text{peck}} D$) whenever $L_h / D > 2.2$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Clean-Room CAM Seed Trajectory Synthesis}
\label{sec:cam_seeds}
Each discrete operation $o \in \mathcal{S}$ initializes a collision-free baseline trajectory $\bar{\boldsymbol{\tau}}^{(o)}$ via one of six clean-room geometric generators (\texttt{cam.strategies}, \texttt{cam.arbitrary\_\allowbreak cad\_\allowbreak ops}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Datum Facing \& Boss Contouring:} Generates bidirectional zig-zag raster passes with lead-in/out extensions beyond stock XY bounds ($a_e = 0.68 D$) for top/hat facing, and exterior offset perimeter passes for boss profiling.
  \item \textbf{Euclidean-EDT 2.5D Level-Set \& Persistent-IPW Rest Clearing:} For arbitrary non-convex STEP/STL pockets, islands, and ribs at axial slice $z_\ell$, we rasterize the forbidden part mask $\mathcal{O}(z_\ell) = \{(x,y) : z_{\text{tgt}}(x,y) > z_\ell + \varepsilon_z\}$ at pitch $\Delta x$ and compute the exact 2D Euclidean Distance Transform $d_{\text{EDT}}(x,y)$ outside $\mathcal{O}(z_\ell)$. To guarantee zero chordal facet gouging on curved boundaries, minimum cutter-center clearance is set to $d_0 = R + \delta_{\text{stock}} + 0.55\Delta x$. Sub-cell Marching Squares extracts nested iso-contours $\{d_{\text{EDT}} = d_0 + m\,a_e\}_{m \ge 0}$ ordered inside-out (or outside-in from open billet edges). For rest-finishing, contours are pruned to active regions where persistent IPW exceeds slice depth ($z_{\text{IPW}}(x,y) - z_\ell > \varepsilon_z$). Enclosed contours enter via a $2.5^\circ$ helical ramp starting $0.5\text{ mm}$ above the \emph{local} IPW ceiling $\max_{\mathcal{N}} z_{\text{IPW}}$, and non-adjacent loops link at verified local IPW clearance.
  \item \textbf{Trochoidal Adaptive Channel Clearing:} Advances a guiding medial centerline by pitch $p_{\text{troch}} \le 0.22 D$ superimposed with circular trochoidal loops so instantaneous radial immersion remains bounded in narrow slots~\cite{rauch2009trochoidal}.
  \item \textbf{Inverse-Offset 3D Ball-Endmill Scallop Surfacing:} Given a 3D freeform B-spline region $\Omega_{\text{3D}}$ and a ball endmill of radius $R_b = D/2$, parallel finishing stepover is chosen from target cusp height $h_{\text{scal}}$ via $s = \sqrt{8 R_b h_{\text{scal}}}$. To guarantee exact gouge-free contact across steep walls and saddle curvature~\cite{choi1998sculptured}, the cutter-tip height $z_{\text{CL}}(x,y)$ is evaluated via the exact inverse-offset envelope over disk $\xi^2 + \eta^2 \le R_b^2$:
  \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
      z_{\text{CL}}(x, y) = \max_{\xi^2 + \eta^2 \le R_b^2} \Big[&z_{\text{tgt}}(x + \xi, y + \eta) \\
      &+ \sqrt{R_b^2 - \xi^2 - \eta^2} - R_b\Big],
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:ball_cl}
  \end{equation}
  clipped strictly to the eroded interior of $\Omega_{\text{3D}}$ and clamped below by the surrounding pocket floor $z_{\text{floor}}(\Omega_{\text{3D}})$ so the spherical flank never undercuts adjacent planar shelves.
  \item \textbf{Helical Bore Interpolation, \texttt{G83} Drilling, Countersinking, \& $45^\circ$ Chamfering:} Counterbores and flat-bottom bores interpolate $(x_h + r_b\cos\theta,\, y_h + r_b\sin\theta,\, z_0 - \frac{p_z}{2\pi}\theta)$ at radius $r_b = \frac{1}{2}(D_{\text{bore}} - D)$ and helical pitch $p_z \le \min(0.25 D, 1.2\text{ mm})$ followed by a $360^\circ$ flat cleanup pass; spot drills and countersinks dwell briefly at exact cone depth; \texttt{G83} peck cycles expand into explicit plunge/retract/re-entry segments for exact simulator timing; and $90^\circ$ chamfer mills offset laterally by $\Delta r_{\text{ch}}$ and axially by $-\big(\Delta r_{\text{ch}} + w_{\text{ch}}\big)$ so the $45^\circ$ bevel engages away from the dead tool tip.
  \item \textbf{Internal Climb Helical Thread Milling:} Right-hand internal threads start at hole center at $z_{\text{bot}}$, arc tangentially onto major-thread radius $r_{\text{thr}} = \frac{1}{2}(D_{\text{major}} - D_{\text{tool}})$, climb-mill upward $\big(x_h + r_{\text{thr}}\cos\theta,\, y_h + r_{\text{thr}}\sin\theta,\, z_{\text{bot}} + \frac{P}{2\pi}\theta\big)$ synchronized to exact thread pitch $P$, and arc back to center before retracting.
\end{itemize}

\section{Differentiable Cutting Simulation, Blended Kinematics, and Co-Optimization}
\label{sec:diffsim_methods}

\subsection{3D Swept-Cylinder SDF and Smooth-to-Hard STE Continuation}
For full 3D volumetric verification and first-order policy/trajectory benchmarking, the stock billet is represented on a 3D Eulerian grid $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ of pitch $\Delta x$ with signed distance field $\phi_t : \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$ after segment $t \in \{0,\dots,T\}$ ($\phi_t(\mathbf{x}) < 0$ inside solid metal). For a cutter of radius $R$, flute length $L_{\text{flute}}$, and holder radius $R_{\text{holder}} > R$ sweeping from $\mathbf{p}_{t-1}$ to $\mathbf{p}_t$ ($\Delta\mathbf{p}_t = \mathbf{p}_t - \mathbf{p}_{t-1}$), let $\tau^\star(\mathbf{x}) = \operatorname{clip}\!\big(\frac{(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_{t-1})\cdot \Delta\mathbf{p}_t}{\|\Delta\mathbf{p}_t\|_2^2 + \varepsilon}, 0, 1\big)$ and $\mathbf{c}_t(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{p}_{t-1} + \tau^\star(\mathbf{x})\Delta\mathbf{p}_t$. The swept flute SDF is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \psi_{\text{tool}}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{p}_{t-1}, \mathbf{p}_t) =\;& \max\Big(
      \|\mathbf{x}^{xy} - \mathbf{c}_t^{xy}(\mathbf{x})\|_2 - R, \\
      &c_t^z(\mathbf{x}) - x^z,\; x^z - \big(c_t^z(\mathbf{x}) + L_{\text{flute}}\big)\Big).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:swept_tool}
\end{equation}
Hard Boolean subtraction updates $\phi_t^{\text{hard}}(\mathbf{x}) = \max\!\big(\phi_{t-1}^{\text{hard}}(\mathbf{x}), -\psi_{\text{tool}}(\mathbf{x})\big)$. To obtain dense spatial derivatives across stock boundaries without low-stiffness volumetric bias, we pair exact Boolean evaluation with a stabilized log-sum-exp surrogate at stiffness $k > 0$:
\begin{equation}
  \phi_t^{(k)}(\mathbf{x}) = m + \frac{1}{k}\log\!\Big(e^{k(\phi_{t-1}^{(k)}(\mathbf{x}) - m)} + e^{k(-\psi_{\text{tool}}(\mathbf{x}) - m)}\Big),
  \label{eq:soft_csg}
\end{equation}
where $m = \max(\phi_{t-1}^{(k)}(\mathbf{x}), -\psi_{\text{tool}}(\mathbf{x}))$ and occupancy is $\sigma_k(\phi) = (1 + e^{k\phi})^{-1}$. Across step $n \in \{0,\dots,N-1\}$, we anneal $k(n)$ linearly from $k_{\min}=25$ to $k_{\max}=90$ inside a \textbf{Straight-Through Estimator (STE)}~\cite{bengio2013estimating}:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{STE}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}; k(n)) = \mathcal{L}_{\text{hard}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) + \Big(\mathcal{L}_{k(n)}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) - \operatorname{sg}\!\big[\mathcal{L}_{k(n)}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\big]\Big),
  \label{eq:ste}
\end{equation}
so model selection and feasibility checks use exact Boolean $\mathcal{L}_{\text{hard}}$, while reverse-mode Taichi kernels propagate $\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\mathcal{L}_{k(n)}$.

\subsection{Blended CNC Kinematics and Exact Junction-Velocity Gradients}
\label{sec:blended_kinematics}
Modern CNC lookahead interpolators avoid full stops (\texttt{G61}) at gentle \texttt{G1} junctions by blending velocity vectors within path tolerance (\texttt{RS274} \texttt{G64 P0.05}, Haas \texttt{G187 P2 E0.05})~\cite{erkorkmaz2001highspeed,kramer2000rs274ngc}. Given segment chord $\ell_k = \|\Delta\mathbf{p}_k\|_2$, unit tangent $\hat{\mathbf{d}}_k = \Delta\mathbf{p}_k / \ell_k$, commanded speed $v_k$ ($f_k$ on \texttt{G1}, $v_{\text{rapid}}$ on \texttt{G0}), and velocity vector $\mathbf{v}_k = v_k \hat{\mathbf{d}}_k$, acceleration-bounded transition duration over axis limit $a_{\max}$ yields
\begin{equation}
  \tau_k(\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{f}) = \frac{\sqrt{\ell_k^2 + \varepsilon_L^2} - \varepsilon_L}{v_k} + \frac{\sqrt{\|\mathbf{v}_k - \mathbf{v}_{k-1}\|_2^2 + \varepsilon_v^2} - \varepsilon_v}{2\,a_{\max}},
  \label{eq:cycle_time}
\end{equation}
plus terminal stop $\|\mathbf{v}_M\|_2 / (2 a_{\max})$ and total time $T_{\text{cycle}} = \sum_{k=1}^M \tau_k$.

A crucial subtlety arises on dense Marching-Squares and 3D surfacing polylines with hundreds of short chords ($\ell_k \sim 0.5\text{--}2\text{ mm}$): boosting $v_k$ while ignoring the second term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cycle_time} inflates $\|\mathbf{v}_k - \mathbf{v}_{k-1}\|_2 / (2 a_{\max})$ at every corner turn, \emph{increasing} true cycle time! Differentiating $T_{\text{cycle}}$ exactly with respect to each station speed $v_k$ couples every segment to both its predecessor $k-1$ and successor $k+1$:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \frac{\partial T_{\text{cycle}}}{\partial v_k} =\;& -\frac{\sqrt{\ell_k^2 + \varepsilon_L^2} - \varepsilon_L}{v_k^2} \\
    &+ \frac{(\mathbf{v}_k - \mathbf{v}_{k-1})\cdot \hat{\mathbf{d}}_k}{2\,a_{\max}\sqrt{\|\mathbf{v}_k - \mathbf{v}_{k-1}\|_2^2 + \varepsilon_v^2}} \\
    &+ \frac{(\mathbf{v}_k - \mathbf{v}_{k+1})\cdot \hat{\mathbf{d}}_k}{2\,a_{\max}\sqrt{\|\mathbf{v}_{k+1} - \mathbf{v}_k\|_2^2 + \varepsilon_v^2}}.
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:junction_grad}
\end{equation}
For collinear segments ($\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{k-1} = \hat{\mathbf{d}}_k = \hat{\mathbf{d}}_{k+1}$), telescopic cancellation leaves pure traversal speedup $-\ell_k/v_k^2$; at sharp turns ($\hat{\mathbf{d}}_k \cdot \hat{\mathbf{d}}_{k+1} < 1$), positive junction sensitivity automatically decelerates $v_k$ \emph{before} the corner when $\ell_k$ is short relative to $v_k^2 / a_{\max}$ while sprinting on straightaways.

\subsection{Chronological 2.5D Dexel CWE, Chip Thinning, and Air Shortcutting}
\label{sec:cwe_physics}
To optimize directly for constant undeformed chip thickness and minimal cycle time (\texttt{SubsegmentedCWEModel} and \texttt{optimize\_\allowbreak arbitrary\_\allowbreak cwe\_\allowbreak plan}), long \texttt{G1} moves are subdivided to $\le 2.0\text{ mm}$ stations ($k=1,\dots,M$) and swept chronologically over the $0.25\text{ mm}$ 2.5D dexel grid $z_{\text{stock}}$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Endcap-Corrected Radial Immersion and Martellotti Chip Thinning:} Because sweeping a disk of diameter $D$ along a short chord $\ell_k^{xy}$ carves both a rectangular swath and circular endcaps, dividing removed volume $\Delta V_k$ by $\ell_k^{xy}$ alone overestimates radial width on short segments. Normalizing by effective swept length $\ell_k^{\text{eff}} = \ell_k^{xy} + \kappa_{\text{cap}} D$ ($\kappa_{\text{cap}} = 0.60$) yields $a_{e,k} = \operatorname{clip}\!\big(\frac{\Delta V_k}{a_{p,k}\,\ell_k^{\text{eff}}}, 0, D\big)$ and fractional immersion $u_k = a_{e,k}/D$. Actual undeformed chip thickness~\cite{martellotti1941milling} is then
  \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
      \psi(u_k) &= \begin{cases}
        \sqrt{4 u_k (1 - u_k)}, & 0 < u_k < 0.5, \\
        1, & u_k \ge 0.5,
      \end{cases} \\
      h_{\text{ex},k} &= \frac{f_k}{f_{\text{tooth}}}\,\psi(u_k).
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:chipload}
  \end{equation}
  Forward evaluation uses exact hard dexel state; reverse-mode STE spatial gradients $\partial \Delta V_k / \partial \mathbf{p}$ and analytical junction/chipload gradients $\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{CWE}} / \partial f_k$ adjust lateral engagements and per-station feeds so $h_{\text{ex},k} \to h_{\text{opt}}$ on engaged cuts while zero-engagement air moves accelerate up to $f_{\max}$.
  \item \textbf{Full-Radius-Verified Empty-Air Shortcutting:} After continuous STE-Adam convergence, contiguous runs of horizontal \texttt{G1} stations verified against the chronological dexel IPW to remove $\Delta V_k = 0$ are tested for shortcut replacement: if sweeping the full physical cutter envelope ($R + 0.06\text{ mm}$) along a direct chord or lifted \texttt{G0} clearance bridge clears all current $z_{\text{stock}}$ and target walls, the redundant air loop is collapsed into a fast bridge—yielding substantial savings even when cutting feeds are held strictly identical across methods.
\end{enumerate}

For 3D SDF benchmarking (\textbf{Exp1--Exp4}), the composite objective combines normalized cycle time, target gouge/residual, Peak MRR barrier $\mathcal{B}_{\text{MRR}} = w_{\text{MRR}} \sum_{t=1}^T \max(0, \text{MRR}_t / \text{MRR}_{\max} - 1)^2$, 2D wall-clearance $\mathcal{B}_{\text{wall}}$, cylindrical holder $\mathcal{B}_{\text{holder}}$, and rapid-stock $\mathcal{B}_{\text{rapid}}$ barriers:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{3D}} =\;& w_T \frac{T_{\text{cycle}}}{T_{\text{nom}}} + w_g \bar{V}_{\text{gouge}}^2 + w_r \bar{V}_{\text{res}}^2 \\
    &+ \mathcal{B}_{\text{MRR}} + \mathcal{B}_{\text{wall}} + \mathcal{B}_{\text{holder}} + \mathcal{B}_{\text{rapid}},
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}
while CWE co-optimization (\textbf{Exp5--Exp7}) minimizes $\mathcal{L}_{\text{CWE}} = \frac{T_{\text{cycle}}}{T_{\text{nom}}} + w_h \sum_{k \in \mathcal{E}} \frac{\tau_k}{T_{\text{nom}}}\big(\frac{h_{\text{ex},k} - h_{\text{eff},k}}{h_{\text{opt}}}\big)^2 + \mathcal{B}_{\text{MRR}} + \mathcal{B}_{\text{res}} + \mathcal{B}_{\text{smooth}}$.

\subsection{Drift-Free Parameterizations, RL Policies, Fast Overfitting, and 6-Dialect Post}
\label{sec:methods}
\textbf{Wall-Pinned Drift-Free Parameterization:}
Perturbing relative steps $\Delta\mathbf{p}_t$ causes $O(T)$ random-walk drift. Instead, we pin axial heights $\bar{p}_i^z$ and bound horizontal waypoint offsets by exact analytical Euclidean wall clearance $d_{\text{wall}}(\bar{\mathbf{p}}_i)$:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \mathbf{p}_i^{xy}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) &= \bar{\mathbf{p}}_i^{xy} + r_i \tanh(\mathbf{u}_{xy,i}), \\
    r_i &= \operatorname{clip}\!\big(d_{\text{wall}}(\bar{\mathbf{p}}_i) - \delta_{\text{pin}},\, 0,\, r_{\max}\big),
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:joint_spline}
\end{equation}
using either clamped cubic B-splines $\mathbf{u}_{xy} = \mathbf{B}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{pos}}$ ($K_p \le 40$, $r_{\max} = 1.25\text{ mm}$) in 3D or vertical-column waypoint groups ($r_{\max} = 3.0\text{ mm}$) keeping vertical plunges strictly vertical in 2.5D CWE, paired with sigmoid feeds $f_k \in [f_{\min}, 0.995\,v_{\max}]$.

\textbf{Generalized Residual RL vs.\ Fast Inference-Time Trajectory Overfitting:}
DiffCAM supports three deployment modes:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Direct Per-Part Differentiable Optimization (\texttt{STE-Adam} / \texttt{DiffCAM-Chipload}):} Optimizes $\boldsymbol{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ ($D \approx 250\text{--}650$) directly on the input part's CAM seed with zero neural training.
  \item \textbf{Amortized Offline RL (\texttt{DiffPolicy-SHAC} / \texttt{Hard-PPO}):} Trains a compact two-layer $64$--$64$ $\tanh$ MLP $\boldsymbol{\pi}_\psi(\mathbf{a}_k \mid \mathbf{s}_k)$ offline on development parts—taking local chord length, turn angle, normalized wall clearance, nominal engagement $u_k$, and alloy/tool context as state $\mathbf{s}_k$—via analytical backprop-through-physics (\texttt{SHAC}~\cite{xu2022accelerated}) or clipped policy gradients with GAE (\texttt{PPO}~\cite{schulman2017proximal,schulman2016gae}), inferring $\boldsymbol{\theta}_0 = \boldsymbol{\pi}_{\psi^\star}(\mathbf{S}(\mathcal{P}_{\text{test}}))$ in $<\!3.5\text{ ms}$.
  \item \textbf{Hybrid Warm-Start Inference-Time Overfitting (\texttt{SHAC + DiffCAM-Overfit-$K$}):} Detaches the MLP at test time, sets $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(0)} \leftarrow \boldsymbol{\pi}_{\psi^\star}(\mathbf{S}(\mathcal{P}_{\text{test}}))$, and takes $K \in \{4,\dots,32\}$ analytical CWE STE-Adam updates directly in $\mathbb{R}^D$ plus air shortcutting—eliminating policy approximation error in sub-second wall time where zero-order RL fine-tuning stalls under $\mathcal{O}(D/\sigma^2)$ variance.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{6-Dialect Multi-Setup Post-Processing and 3D Tool-Library WebGL Simulator:}
Clean-room post-processors (\texttt{cam.post\_processors}) compile multi-setup programs across 6 industrial controllers (\texttt{Haas NGC}, \texttt{LinuxCNC RS274}, \texttt{FANUC 31i}, \texttt{FANUC Robodrill}, \texttt{Makino Pro 5}, and \texttt{Mazak SmoothG} ISO), emitting safe start blocks, optional \texttt{T99 G65 P9995/P9811/P9814} Renishaw WIPS touch-probing headers, \texttt{Tn M6} / \texttt{G43 Hn} tool-length compensation, \texttt{G187}/\texttt{G05.1} smoothing codes, \texttt{M00} Op1$\to$Op2 flip stops (\texttt{G54}$\to$\texttt{G55}), and exact bi-directional segment-to-line maps. Our web interface (\texttt{scripts/serve\_simulator.py}, \texttt{viewer/}) allows users to upload arbitrary \texttt{.step}/\texttt{.stl} parts, switch between US/Metric/Custom Tool Cribs, tune aggressiveness and alloys, and inspect 3-way side-by-side stock carving with realistic 3D holder + flute/drill/ball/chamfer/thread-mill assemblies synchronized line-by-line with live 6-dialect G-code.

\section{Experimental Setup, Split Protocol, and Metrics}

(2) \textbf{Multi-Tool \texttt{.stl} Suite (\textbf{Exp6}):} Four multi-feature 3D binary \texttt{.stl} solids across Al 6061-T6, Al 7075-T6, and Mild Steel 1018.
(3) \textbf{Arbitrary ISO~10303-21 \texttt{.step} Suite (\textbf{Exp7}):} All 10 parts of the industry-standard \texttt{TITAN-1M} through \texttt{TITAN-10M} CNC building-block series (\texttt{example\_parts/Titan-M1.step..M10.step}, authored in inches with $49\text{--}203\text{ mm}$ spans, multi-pocket webs, radiating ribs, blind/through holes, counterbores, $82^\circ$ countersinks, $45^\circ$ chamfers, ANSI Unified internal threads, underside cavities requiring \texttt{G54+G55} flips, and 3D B-spline domes/airfoils), machined in Al 6061-T6 on a Haas VF-2/Mini-Mill envelope ($10{,}000\text{ RPM}$, $15\text{ kW}$, $a_{\max} = 1500\text{ mm/s}^2$).

\textbf{Role of \texttt{dev} vs.\ Held-Out \texttt{test} Splits:}
Direct per-part optimizers (\texttt{STE-Continuation-Adam}, \texttt{DiffCAM-Chipload}, \texttt{CEM/CMA-ES}) use \texttt{dev} solely to freeze hyperparameters and start cold from each unseen \texttt{test} part's CAM seed. Generalized RL policies (\texttt{DiffPolicy-SHAC}, \texttt{Hard-PPO}) train neural weights $\psi^\star$ strictly offline on \texttt{dev} and evaluate zero-shot ($K=0$) or with $K$-step inference-time trajectory overfitting on \texttt{test}. Automated TAMP (\textbf{Exp6--Exp7}) requires zero training split, planning directly from raw \texttt{.stl}/\texttt{.step} geometry.

\textbf{Physical Evaluation Metrics:}
We report: (i) \textbf{Blended Cycle Time $T_{\text{cycle}}$ (s)} (Eq.~\eqref{eq:cycle_time}), decomposed into engaged cutting ($\Delta V_k > 0$), unproductive \texttt{G1} air-cutting ($\Delta V_k = 0$), and \texttt{G0}/ATC swap time ($6.0\text{ s}$/swap); (ii) \textbf{Fair Same-Speed Time $T_{\text{same}}$ vs.\ Optimal-Chipload Time $T_{\text{opt}}$ (s)}, where $T_{\text{same}}$ locks every method to identical baseline cutting feeds $f_{\text{base}}$ to isolate pure geometric and air-shortcut gains; (iii) \textbf{Target Gouge $V_{\text{gouge}}$ and Residual $V_{\text{res}}$ ($\text{mm}^3$)} plus binary vise-jaw, holder, and rapid-stock collision audits; (iv) \textbf{Peak MRR ($\text{mm}^3/\text{s}$) and Overload ($\%$)} above $\text{MRR}_{\max}$; (v) \textbf{Actual Chip Thickness $h_{\text{ex}}$ (mm), RMSE, and Optimal Band ($\%$)} inside $[0.56\,h_{\text{opt}}, 1.26\,h_{\text{opt}}]$; and (vi) \textbf{Gradient SNR, Hard-FD Cosine, $L_1$ Occupancy Drift, and CAMotics Watertight STL $\Delta V$ ($\%$)}.

\subsection{Exp1: Surrogate Conditioning and Perturbed Reachability}
\textbf{Motivation:} Smooth CSG surrogates trade wide gradient support at low stiffness $k$ against Boolean exactness at high $k$. Across $k \in \{10, 20, 40, 80, 160\}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:exp1}), analytical gradient SNR and cosine similarity to central hard finite differences peak at $k \in \{10, 20\}$ ($\text{SNR} = \mathbf{62.19}\text{--}\mathbf{59.24}$, $\cos\theta = \mathbf{0.737}\text{--}\mathbf{0.758}$) and stay well-conditioned through $k = 80\text{--}160$ ($\text{SNR} = 32.14\text{--}30.53$, $\cos\theta = 0.47\text{--}0.50$), while soft-to-hard occupancy $L_1$ drift drops from $\mathbf{72.6\%}$ ($k=10$) to $\mathbf{8.7\%}$ ($k=80$) and $\mathbf{3.2\%}$ ($k=160$). Annealing $k: 25 \to 90$ under STE recovers $100\%$ feasibility and $38.7\%$--$41.1\%$ cycle-time savings from perturbed initializations $\|\boldsymbol{\theta}_0\|_\infty \le R \in \{0.0, 0.2, 0.4\}$ ($5.8\times$--$7.6\times$ \texttt{Hard-CMA-ES}).

\subsection{Exp5 \& Exp5b: Fair Same-Speed vs.\ Optimal Chipload and Fast Inference-Time Trajectory Overfitting}
\label{sec:exp5}
\textbf{Motivation:} \textbf{Exp5} re-evaluates all 8 unseen \texttt{test} parts against a physics-calibrated \texttt{Nominal-CAM (@2)} baseline set to the exact \texttt{feeds\_and\_speeds} rate ($f_{\text{base}} = 11.69\text{ mm/s}$, $h_{\text{opt}} = 0.0291\text{ mm/tooth}$), separating \emph{Fair Same-Speed} ($T_{\text{same}}$) from \emph{Optimal-Chipload} ($T_{\text{opt}}$) performance (Table~\ref{tab:exp5_overfit} top, Fig.~\ref{fig:exp5}a). \textbf{Exp5b} tests whether $<\!3.5\text{ ms}$ offline RL warm-starting plus $K \in \{4,\dots,32\}$ analytical trajectory-overfitting steps can achieve test-part optimality faster than test-time RL (Table~\ref{tab:exp5_overfit} bottom, Fig.~\ref{fig:exp5}b).

\subsection{Exp7: Arbitrary ISO~10303-21 STEP Benchmark on \texttt{TITAN-1M--10M}}
\label{sec:exp7}
\textbf{Motivation:} To test true ``slicer-like'' end-to-end generality on real-world multi-setup, freeform, and threaded engineering CAD parts, \textbf{Exp7} evaluates DiffCAM directly on all 10 unaltered ISO~10303-21 \texttt{.step} files of the \texttt{TITAN-1M}--\texttt{TITAN-10M} aerospace building-block curriculum (\texttt{Titan-M1.step}--\texttt{Titan-M10.step}) constrained to our US Imperial Shop Tool Crib.

Figure~\ref{fig:exp7} and Table~\ref{tab:exp7_titan} summarize all 10 parts ($17.0\text{ s}$ mean end-to-end STEP parsing, setup scoring, TAMP sequencing, and CWE optimization wall time per part):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Automated 6-Axis $SO(3)$ Reorientation \& Multi-Setup Soft-Jaw Flipping:} Six parts (\texttt{M2, M3, M5, M6, M9, M10}) authored with primary features along $+Y$ are automatically rotated $+Y \mapsto +Z$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:setup_score}. Four parts (\texttt{M4, M7, M8, M10}) with top-occluded underside cavities or full-depth profiled silhouettes automatically synthesize two-setup \texttt{Op1 (G54)} $\to$ \texttt{M00} $\to$ \texttt{Op2 (G55)} plans, facing off the sacrificial carrier hat and finishing underside pockets/counterbores in flipped coordinates (Eq.~\eqref{eq:op2_flip}) with zero vise-jaw collisions.
  \item \textbf{Multi-Class Crib Sequencing, Thread Milling, \& 3D Ball Scallop:} Across the 10 parts, TAMP sequences $6$--$11$ unique US tools ($7$--$13$ operations, $4{,}743$--$19{,}927$ Haas NGC lines/part), combining $2''$ face mills, $5/8''$ roughers, $1/4''$ rest/helical-bore flat mills, $1/4''$ ball endmills for gouge-free inverse-offset 3D B-spline surfacing on \texttt{Titan-M9} ($6$ dome regions) and \texttt{Titan-M10} ($8$ airfoil regions), $90^\circ$ spot drills, wire/fractional \texttt{G83} drills, $82^\circ$ countersinks, $45^\circ$ chamfer mills, and internal climb thread mills (\texttt{M3, M4, M5, M10}). On \texttt{Titan-M1}, when an exact $\varnothing 2.06\text{ mm}$ drill and \texttt{\#2-56} micro-thread mill are absent from the standard crib, TAMP safely falls back to a \texttt{\#47} ($0.0785''$) wire pilot drill and emits a clear crib warning.
  \item \textbf{Consistent Gains from Exact \texttt{G187} Junction Gradients \& Zero Gouge:} Every single part ($10/10$) satisfies $T_{\text{opt}} < T_{\text{same}} < T_{\text{naive}}$ with $\mathbf{0.000\text{ mm}^3}$ target gouge and $0$ vise, holder, or rapid collisions. Fair matched-speed air shortcutting reduces mean cycle time from $2363.2\text{ s} \to \mathbf{1488.8\text{ s}}$ ($\mathbf{-36.3\%}$), while joint CWE + exact \texttt{G187} junction-velocity optimization (Eq.~\eqref{eq:junction_grad}) reaches $\mathbf{1362.5\text{ s}}$ ($\mathbf{-41.6\%}$ mean, up to $\mathbf{-54.1\%}$ on 3D-sculptured \texttt{Titan-M9}), compiling cleanly across all $60/60$ 6-dialect NC programs.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\textbf{Limitations and Remaining Frontiers.}
While DiffCAM advances end-to-end 3-axis automation from raw ISO~10303-21 \texttt{.step} and \texttt{.stl} CAD solids through 6-sided $SO(3)$ setup orientation, \texttt{Op1 (G54)} / \texttt{Op2 (G55)} soft-jaw flipping, US/Metric crib-constrained tool sequencing, 3D ball-endmill scallop surfacing, and thread milling, four open research frontiers remain: (1) \emph{Off-axis undercuts and 3+2 / simultaneous 5-axis indexing} for features inaccessible along $\pm Z$ without T-slot/dovetail cutters or rotary trunnions; (2) \emph{Coupled frequency-domain regenerative chatter stability lobes and thin-wall workpiece compliance}~\cite{altintas2012manufacturing} beyond static cantilever cutter deflection; (3) \emph{Joint 3D stepover-and-$z$ freeform trajectory optimization} over high-resolution multi-dexel fields; and (4) \emph{Instrumented physical Haas VMC machining trials} measuring spindle load, cycle time, and surface finish $R_a$.

\textbf{Conclusion.}
We challenged the manual paradigm of 3-axis CNC programming with \textbf{DiffCAM}, unifying bi-level multi-setup TAMP over a 673-record clean-room tool catalog and physics-based \texttt{feeds\_and\_speeds} with classical CAM trajectory initialization, smooth-to-hard differentiable cutting simulation, and trajectory-policy co-optimization. Across 7 experimental suites—culminating in $10/10$ gouge-free multi-tool machining of arbitrary \texttt{TITAN-1M--10M} STEP solids ($41.6\%$ mean cycle-time reduction)—DiffCAM demonstrates that subtractive 3-axis CNC milling can approach the automated, upload-and-cut accessibility of 3D printing.
