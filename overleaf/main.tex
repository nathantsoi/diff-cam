\title{Automated End-to-End 3-Axis CNC Machining: Task-Level Multi-Tool Planning, Differentiable Cutting Simulation, and Trajectory-Policy Co-Optimization}

\author{Author Names Omitted for Anonymous Review}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Advances in fused-deposition 3D printing have made additive fabrication accessible to non-experts: a user drops a digital mesh into slicer software, which automatically plans and executes trajectories in $\mathbb{R}^4$ $(x, y, z, \text{extrude})$. That accessibility has not transferred to 3-axis Computer Numerical Control (CNC) milling. Although 3-axis CNC trajectories inhabit a similarly compact continuous action space, subtractive machining requires first solving a combinatorial upstream task-planning problem—selecting raw stock material and dimensions, workholding orientation and clamping, multi-class cutting/drilling/threading tool libraries, feature-bound toolpath topologies, and narrow physically admissible bands of feeds and speeds—followed by long-horizon trajectory optimization through discontinuous Boolean material removal. Today, this hierarchy requires hours of manual configuration by expert CNC programmers. We challenge this paradigm with \textbf{DiffCAM}, an MIT-licensed, end-to-end automated 3-axis CNC machining framework that couples (1) task-and-motion-planning (TAMP) setup, workholding, and multi-tool operation selection over a 646-record clean-room tool catalog and physics-based \texttt{feeds\_and\_speeds} solver, (2) classical clean-room CAM trajectory initialization across facing, pocketing, trochoidal adaptive clearing, contouring, and peck drilling, and (3) dual-mode differentiable cutting simulation combining 3D swept-cylinder signed distance fields (SDFs) with Straight-Through Estimator (STE) stiffness continuation and chronological 2.5D cutter-workpiece engagement (CWE) radial chip-thinning mechanics. Across 16 single-tool benchmark tasks ($B = 64$ simulator calls), smooth-to-hard continuation cuts cycle time by $\mathbf{42.0 \pm 13.5\%}$ at $100\%$ hard feasibility ($4\times$ evolutionary search), while first-order \texttt{DiffPolicy-SHAC} doubles the sample efficiency of zero-order \texttt{Hard-PPO} ($\mathbf{37.0\%}$ vs.\ $\mathbf{18.5\%}$). Under strict matched-speed and optimal-chipload CWE evaluation (\textbf{Exp5}), \texttt{DiffCAM-Chipload} saves up to $7.9\%$ cycle time at \emph{identical} cutting feedrate via stock-verified air-detour shortcutting and $\mathbf{14.9\%}$ overall while maintaining $\mathbf{97.7\%}$ of engaged cutting inside the optimal undeformed chip-thickness band ($h_{\text{ex}}$ RMSE $0.0053\text{ mm}$). Furthermore, warm-starting from zero-shot \texttt{SHAC} ($3.5\text{ ms}$) or clean-room CAM seeds and rapidly overfitting directly to the test inference trajectory for $K \in \{8,\dots,32\}$ analytical CWE steps (\textbf{Exp5b}) achieves part-specific optimality in sub-second wall time ($0.48\text{--}1.04\text{ s}$, $95.6\text{--}98.5\%$ optimal chipload)—a regime unreachable by test-time RL under comparable budgets. Finally, our automated multi-tool TAMP pipeline (\textbf{Exp6}) maps raw watertight \texttt{.stl} parts across three alloys directly into verified 4-tool Haas Next-Gen Control programs (\texttt{O02001}--\texttt{O02004}, up to $36.7\%$ total / $43.7\%$ cutting-time reduction) with $0.00\text{ mm}^3$ gouge, alongside $48/48$ six-dialect post-processor and \texttt{CAMotics v1.2.0} watertight STL certification.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Roboticists in the past have often dismissed the robotic challenges of manufacturing because the factory domain appears relatively well-defined and structured rather than addressing open-ended, general-purpose manipulation tasks. Yet manifesting physical objects from digital designs via machine tools has historically required deep artistic and technical expertise. Advances in desktop 3D printing have demonstrated that technical expertise in manufacturing processes is not inherently required to realize digital objects in the physical world: a user no longer needs to understand toolpaths, kinematics, or robotics, and can instead simply drop a digital model into a 3D printer's slicer software, which automatically plans and executes the trajectories required to extrude plastic in the shape of the part.

However, the plug-and-play accessibility of 3D printing software has not transferred to more complex and capable manufacturing processes, such as 3-axis CNC milling machines. At the continuous motion level, the instantaneous action space for a typical fused-deposition modeling (FDM) 3D printer is $\mathbb{R}^4$ $(x, y, z, \text{extrude})$, and the trajectory action space for 3-axis CNC machining is superficially similar—$\mathbb{R}^4$ $(x, y, z, \text{spindle/cutting speed})$. Relative to 3D printing, CNC machining allows the fast and repeatable creation of high-strength functional parts out of both common and exotic structural materials. However, because machining is a \emph{subtractive} process—in which a rotating cutter permanently carves away solid metal and every motion irreversibly alters future cutting loads~\cite{altintas2012manufacturing,suh2008cnc}—vastly more upstream planning is required prior to choosing a continuous trajectory:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Stock and Workholding:} One must first select a piece of raw material, called \emph{stock} (including alloy composition and billet dimensions), choose the orientation of the stock inside the CNC work envelope, and determine how it is rigidly clamped down against cutting forces, called \emph{workholding} (e.g., parallel vise jaws that themselves become collision obstacles).
  \item \textbf{Multi-Class Tool Libraries and Operation Topologies:} Whereas FDM 3D printers typically operate with a single nozzle diameter and one filament corresponding to a single primitive operation (extruding plastic), cost- and time-efficiently removing material from metal stock requires a diverse \emph{library of tools} spanning distinct classes of operations: \emph{cutting tools} (flat, bull-nose, and ball end mills, face mills), \emph{hole-making tools} (spot drills, twist drills, reamers), and \emph{threading tools} (taps, thread mills). Each tool is paired with specialized parameterized \emph{toolpaths}: for instance, drills can plunge through shallow stock in a single motion or execute small incremental cutting motions and periodically retract to evacuate chips before plunging deeper (peck drilling); cutting tools can slice a thin datum layer off the top of the stock via a \emph{facing} toolpath, hog out bulk material rapidly at lower precision via a \emph{roughing} or trochoidal toolpath~\cite{held1991pocket,rauch2009trochoidal}, or clean tight concave corners left behind by larger tools via rest-machining.
  \item \textbf{Narrow Admissible Feeds and Speeds:} Every combination of workpiece alloy, cutter substrate, flute geometry, and local radial/axial engagement admits only a narrow physical operating window for the rotational speed of the spindle relative to linear motion against the workpiece, known as \emph{feeds and speeds}~\cite{martellotti1941milling,altintas2012manufacturing}. Feeding too slowly below minimum chip thickness causes rubbing and work-hardening; feeding too aggressively exceeds chip-evacuation, spindle torque, or cantilever tool-deflection limits and snaps the cutter.
\end{enumerate}

The combination of stock material, workholding, tool selection, toolpath topology, and feeds and speeds creates a massive hierarchical planning space that must be resolved before optimizing any continuous trajectory. Today, people solve the complexity of CNC machining in an overwhelmingly manual way: expert CNC programmers spend many hours in visual computer-aided manufacturing (CAM) interfaces manually configuring stock billets, fixture clearances, tool-library assemblies, feature-associated parameterized toolpaths, and handbook or calculator-derived feeds and speeds.

We challenge this manual paradigm and propose that 3-axis CNC machining should be solvable end-to-end in an automated way—at a quality level that currently demands hours of expert programmer time—using modern learning-based and gradient-based approaches that combine task-level setup planning prior to trajectory optimization with classical CAM trajectory initialization and differentiable cutting simulation. Realizing this vision requires overcoming three fundamental technical barriers at the physics-and-learning interface:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Discontinuous Boolean Material Removal and Surrogate Drift:} Subtracting a swept cutter volume $\psi_{\text{tool}}$ from an in-process workpiece (IPW) signed distance field $\phi_t$ via $\phi_{t+1} = \max(\phi_t, -\psi_{\text{tool}})$~\cite{vanhook1986dexel,shapiro2007rfunctions} yields identically zero spatial gradients in empty air and non-differentiable kinks at stock boundaries, while naive smooth relaxations suffer volumetric occupancy drift~\cite{suh2022differentiable,metz2021gradients} and relative waypoint deltas accumulate $O(T)$ drift across $T \sim 37\text{--}370$ segments.
  \item \textbf{Controller- and Chipload-Blind Kinematics:} Optimizing path length at constant speed ignores physical CNC corner blending (\texttt{RS274} \texttt{G64}, Haas \texttt{G187})~\cite{erkorkmaz2001highspeed,kramer2000rs274ngc}, whereas optimizing volumetric removal without radial chip-thinning mechanics~\cite{martellotti1941milling} distorts actual undeformed chip thickness $h_{\text{ex}}$ during partial-immersion cuts.
  \item \textbf{Per-Part Planning Latency vs.\ Generalized RL Suboptimality:} Training an amortized RL policy yields millisecond zero-shot inference on unseen parts, whereas direct per-part differentiable optimization tailors every waypoint and feedrate to the exact part geometry—raising the question of whether fast test-time trajectory overfitting can unify both regimes.
\end{enumerate}

To address both the upstream setup bottleneck and downstream trajectory physics, we present \textbf{DiffCAM} (Fig.~\ref{fig:hero}), an MIT-licensed automated 3-axis machining stack. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Automated Multi-Tool Task-and-Motion Planning (TAMP):} We build a 100\% clean-room MIT catalog of $646$ tools and holders across $15$ library groups and $32$ entity types (\texttt{StandardToolLibrary}), paired with a clean-room \texttt{feeds\_and\_speeds} physics engine and an \texttt{.stl}-driven TAMP planner (\texttt{cam.tamp\_planner}) that automatically selects stock margins, vise-jaw workholding zones, multi-tool roughing/rest-finishing/spot-drilling/peck-drilling sequences, and persistent-IPW trajectories.
  \item \textbf{Dual-Mode Differentiable Cutting Simulation (3D Swept-SDF \& Chronological 2.5D CWE):} We formulate both (a) a GPU-accelerated Taichi~\cite{hu2020difftaichi} 3D swept-CSG simulator with Straight-Through Estimator (STE)~\cite{bengio2013estimating} stiffness continuation ($k: 25 \to 90$), acceleration-bounded corner blending, and volumetric Peak MRR barriers, and (b) a sub-segmented chronological 2.5D dexel CWE optimizer (\texttt{SubsegmentedCWEModel}) modeling Martellotti radial chip thinning, slotting derating, cantilever deflection, and stock-verified empty-air shortcutting.
  \item \textbf{Controlled RL Benchmark and Fast Inference-Time Trajectory Overfitting:} Across 16 tasks and 8 methods under strict simulator-call parity, we quantify first-order (\texttt{STE-Continuation-Adam}, \texttt{DiffPolicy-SHAC}~\cite{xu2022accelerated}) vs.\ zero-order (\texttt{Hard-PPO}~\cite{schulman2017proximal}, \texttt{Hard-CEM}~\cite{rubinstein2004cem}, \texttt{Hard-CMA-ES}~\cite{hansen2001cmaes}) optimization, separate fair same-speed geometric gains from optimal-chipload gains (\textbf{Exp5}), and show that warm-starting from zero-shot \texttt{SHAC} followed by $K \in \{4,\dots,32\}$ analytical trajectory-overfitting steps (\textbf{Exp5b}) rapidly outperforms both zero-shot RL and test-time PPO fine-tuning.
  \item \textbf{6-Controller G-Code, CAMotics STL, and Live 3-Way WebGL Verification:} Solutions are verified across 6 CNC dialects ($48/48$ pass), containerized \texttt{CAMotics v1.2.0}~\cite{coffland2021camotics} ($8/8$ watertight STLs, $0.00\text{ mm}^3$ gouge), multi-tool Haas NGC packets (\texttt{O02001}--\texttt{O02004}), and an interactive 3-way side-by-side WebGL stock-carving simulator.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) and Feature Recognition.}
Integrated task and motion planning couples discrete symbolic decisions—such as object, grasp, or tool selection—with continuous geometric feasibility and trajectory optimization~\cite{kaelbling2011hpn,garrett2021tamp}. While TAMP has been widely studied in pick-and-place, assembly, and robotic extrusion, subtractive CNC process planning has largely remained split between rule-based CAD feature recognition and manual CAM setup~\cite{suh2008cnc}. DiffCAM brings a TAMP formulation directly to 3-axis subtractive milling: discrete search over a multi-class tool library balances bulk roughing MRR against physical automatic-tool-changer (ATC) swap latency while passing an evolving persistent 2.5D IPW heightfield across consecutive tools so downstream rest-finishing trajectories adapt continuously to upstream stock removal.

\textbf{Geometric CAM, Engagement Control, and Feeds/Speeds.}
Classical pocket and contour milling rely on 2D Voronoi medial-axis offsets~\cite{held1991pocket} and parallel raster passes, which suffer sharp radial immersion spikes in concave corners. Trochoidal and adaptive strategies~\cite{rauch2009trochoidal,altintas2012manufacturing} bound engagement geometrically, while solid verification uses Z-buffer dexels~\cite{vanhook1986dexel}, R-functions~\cite{shapiro2007rfunctions}, or octree isosurfaces~\cite{coffland2021camotics}. Independently, metal-cutting mechanics establishes that when radial width of cut $a_e$ is below cutter radius $R = D/2$, the maximum undeformed chip thickness $h_{\text{ex}}$ thins according to Martellotti's kinematic relation~\cite{martellotti1941milling,altintas2012manufacturing}, requiring elevated feed per tooth $f_z$ to prevent rubbing while respecting spindle power and cantilever beam deflection limits. Prior feedrate schedulers adjust speed along a \emph{frozen} path~\cite{erkorkmaz2001highspeed}; DiffCAM jointly optimizes continuous XY waypoints, stock-verified air shortcuts, and per-station feeds through differentiable CWE and corner-blending kinematics~\cite{kramer2000rs274ngc}.

\textbf{Differentiable Simulation, Amortized RL, and Test-Time Optimization.}
Differentiable physics engines~\cite{hu2020difftaichi,freeman2021brax} enable low-variance first-order trajectory optimization and short-horizon analytic actor-critic (\texttt{SHAC}) policy learning~\cite{xu2022accelerated}. However, discontinuous contact or Boolean clipping can induce gradient variance or surrogate bias~\cite{suh2022differentiable,metz2021gradients}, motivating comparisons with zero-order \texttt{PPO}~\cite{schulman2017proximal,schulman2016gae}, \texttt{CEM}~\cite{rubinstein2004cem}, and \texttt{CMA-ES}~\cite{hansen2001cmaes}. Beyond offline training, our work highlights a key structural advantage of differentiable simulation over model-free RL at deployment: whereas fine-tuning an RL policy on a single test instance requires hundreds of noisy rollouts, warm-starting from an amortized neural policy and taking a handful of analytical simulator gradient steps directly on the inference trajectory eliminates policy approximation error in milliseconds.

\section{Hierarchical Task Planning and Differentiable Cutting Mechanics}
\label{sec:formulation}

\subsection{Task-Level Setup, Clean-Room Tool Library, and Persistent-IPW Planning}
\label{sec:tamp}
Given an input watertight 3D triangle mesh $\mathcal{M} \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ (\texttt{.stl}), workpiece alloy identifier $m_{\text{stock}}$, and machine envelope $\mathcal{E}_{\text{mach}}$, our upstream TAMP module (\texttt{cam.tamp\_planner}) resolves setup and discrete multi-tool sequencing prior to continuous optimization:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Stock, Orientation, and Workholding Synthesis:} Aligning primary feature accessibility with $+Z$, the planner selects billet bounds $[x_{\min}, x_{\max}] \times [y_{\min}, y_{\max}] \times [-L_z, 0]$ incorporating top-facing datum stock $\delta_{\text{top}}$ ($1.0\text{--}1.5\text{ mm}$) and bottom parallel vise-jaw clamping grip $\delta_{\text{grip}}$ ($2.0\text{ mm}$), instantiating explicit forbidden vise-jaw collision boxes $\mathcal{V}_{-Y}, \mathcal{V}_{+Y}$ along the clamped faces.
  \item \textbf{2.5D + Hole Geometric Feature Recognition:} Rasterizing upward-facing facets ($n_z > 0$) onto a $0.25\text{ mm}$ G54 heightfield $z_{\text{tgt}}(x,y)$ extracts top-facing depth, exterior boss steps, cylindrical blind/through holes $\{(x_h, y_h, D_h, z_{\text{top}}, z_{\text{bot}})\}$ (identified via aspect-ratio and $\pi/4$ bounding-box circularity invariants), and enclosed 2.5D pockets/islands along with minimum channel width $w_{\min}$ and concave corner fillet radius $r_{\min}$.
  \item \textbf{Discrete Tool-Library Search with ATC Cost:} Querying our clean-room \texttt{StandardToolLibrary} ($\mathcal{T}$, containing $574$ metric/inch milling, drilling, and threading tools plus $72$ BT30/CAT40/HSK holders across $15$ library groups and $32$ entity types), the planner bounds maximum finishing endmill diameter by $D_{\text{fin}} \le \min(2 r_{\min}, w_{\min} - \delta_c)$ and evaluates whether prepending a larger rigid roughing endmill $D_{\text{rough}} > D_{\text{fin}}$ reduces total machining time once physical automatic-tool-changer swap overhead $t_{\text{ATC}} = 6.0\text{ s}$ is charged:
  \begin{equation}
    J_{\text{task}}(\mathcal{S}) = \sum_{o \in \mathcal{S}} \frac{\widehat{V}_o}{\operatorname{MRR}(m_{\text{stock}}, \tau_o)} + (|\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S})| - 1)\,t_{\text{ATC}},
    \label{eq:tamp_cost}
  \end{equation}
  where $\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{S})$ is the set of unique tools in operation sequence $\mathcal{S}$ and $\operatorname{MRR}(m_{\text{stock}}, \tau_o)$ is computed by our clean-room \texttt{feeds\_and\_speeds} physics solver. Top facing and exterior boss contouring reuse the selected primary endmill ($T_1$) to incur zero extra tool change, followed if beneficial by $T_2$ corner/island rest-finishing, $T_3$ $90^\circ$ spot-drill chamfering, and $T_4$ diameter-matched carbide twist drilling (automatically switching to \texttt{G83} multi-peck chip-clearing cycles whenever hole aspect ratio $L_h / D_h > 2.2$).
  \item \textbf{Persistent In-Process Workpiece (IPW) Propagation:} Rather than optimizing each operation on an idealized fresh block, the evolving 2.5D dexel stock field $z_{\text{IPW}}^{(o)}(x,y)$ carved by operation $o$ is passed directly as the initial stock state $z_{\text{IPW}}^{(o+1)} \leftarrow z_{\text{final}}^{(o)}$ to operation $o+1$, enabling rest-finishing tools to bridge rapidly across already-evacuated pocket interiors and regulate chip load strictly inside uncut corner cusps.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Dual-Mode 3D Swept-Cylinder SDF and STE Continuation}
For general 3D volumetric verification and first-order trajectory/RL benchmarking, we represent the stock billet on a uniform Eulerian grid $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ of pitch $\Delta x$ with signed distance values $\phi_t : \Omega \to \mathbb{R}$ after segment $t \in \{0,\dots,T\}$ ($\phi_t(\mathbf{x}) < 0$ inside material). For a flat-end cutter of radius $R$, flute length $L_{\text{flute}}$, holder clearance $L_{\text{clear}}$, and holder radius $R_{\text{holder}} > R$ sweeping linearly from $\mathbf{p}_{t-1}$ to $\mathbf{p}_t$ ($\Delta\mathbf{p}_t = \mathbf{p}_t - \mathbf{p}_{t-1}$), let $\tau^\star(\mathbf{x}) = \operatorname{clip}\!\big(\frac{(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{p}_{t-1})\cdot \Delta\mathbf{p}_t}{\|\Delta\mathbf{p}_t\|_2^2 + \varepsilon}, 0, 1\big)$ and $\mathbf{c}_t(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{p}_{t-1} + \tau^\star(\mathbf{x})\Delta\mathbf{p}_t$. The swept cutting-flute SDF is
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \psi_{\text{tool}}(\mathbf{x}; \mathbf{p}_{t-1}, \mathbf{p}_t) =\;& \max\Big(
      \|\mathbf{x}^{xy} - \mathbf{c}_t^{xy}(\mathbf{x})\|_2 - R, \\
      &c_t^z(\mathbf{x}) - x^z,\; x^z - \big(c_t^z(\mathbf{x}) + L_{\text{flute}}\big)\Big).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:swept_tool}
\end{equation}
Exact Boolean subtraction updates $\phi_t^{\text{hard}}(\mathbf{x}) = \max\!\big(\phi_{t-1}^{\text{hard}}(\mathbf{x}), -\psi_{\text{tool}}(\mathbf{x})\big)$. To obtain dense analytical derivatives across stock boundaries without suffering low-stiffness volumetric inflation, we pair exact Boolean evaluation with a numerically stabilized log-sum-exp smooth surrogate at stiffness $k > 0$:
\begin{equation}
  \phi_t^{(k)}(\mathbf{x}) = m + \frac{1}{k}\log\!\Big(e^{k(\phi_{t-1}^{(k)}(\mathbf{x}) - m)} + e^{k(-\psi_{\text{tool}}(\mathbf{x}) - m)}\Big),
  \label{eq:soft_csg}
\end{equation}
where $m = \max(\phi_{t-1}^{(k)}(\mathbf{x}), -\psi_{\text{tool}}(\mathbf{x}))$ and smooth occupancy is $\sigma_k(\phi) = (1 + e^{k\phi})^{-1}$. Across optimization step $n \in \{0,\dots,N-1\}$, we anneal $k(n)$ linearly from $k_{\min}=25$ to $k_{\max}=90$ inside a \textbf{Straight-Through Estimator (STE)}~\cite{bengio2013estimating}:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{STE}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}; k(n)) = \mathcal{L}_{\text{hard}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) + \Big(\mathcal{L}_{k(n)}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) - \operatorname{sg}\!\big[\mathcal{L}_{k(n)}(\boldsymbol{\theta})\big]\Big),
  \label{eq:ste}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sg}[\cdot]$ stops gradients. Forward model selection and feasibility checkpointing are thus governed strictly by exact Boolean $\mathcal{L}_{\text{hard}}$, while reverse-mode Taichi kernels propagate $\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \mathcal{L}_{k(n)}$.

\subsection{Blended Acceleration-Bounded CNC Kinematics}
Physical CNC lookahead interpolators avoid full stops (\texttt{G61}) at collinear or gentle \texttt{G1} junctions by blending velocity vectors within a path tolerance (\texttt{RS274} \texttt{G64 P0.05}, Haas \texttt{G187 P2 E0.05})~\cite{erkorkmaz2001highspeed,kramer2000rs274ngc}. Given segment chord $\ell_t = \|\Delta\mathbf{p}_t\|_2$, unit tangent $\hat{\mathbf{d}}_t$, commanded speed $v_t$ ($f_t$ on \texttt{G1}, $v_{\text{rapid}}$ on \texttt{G0}), and velocity vector $\mathbf{v}_t = v_t \hat{\mathbf{d}}_t$, acceleration-bounded junction transition duration over machine limit $a_{\max}$ yields smooth, closed-form segment durations
\begin{equation}
  \tau_t(\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{f}) = \frac{\sqrt{\ell_t^2 + \varepsilon_L^2} - \varepsilon_L}{v_t} + \frac{\sqrt{\|\mathbf{v}_t - \mathbf{v}_{t-1}\|_2^2 + \varepsilon_v^2} - \varepsilon_v}{2\,a_{\max}},
  \label{eq:cycle_time}
\end{equation}
plus terminal deceleration $\|\mathbf{v}_T\|_2 / (2 a_{\max})$, with total cycle time $T_{\text{cycle}} = \sum_{t=1}^T \tau_t(\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{f})$. Equation~\eqref{eq:cycle_time} is analytically differentiable with respect to both waypoint coordinates $\mathbf{p}$ and per-segment speeds $\mathbf{f}$.

\subsection{Chronological 2.5D Cutter-Workpiece Engagement (CWE) and Chipload Physics}
\label{sec:cwe_physics}
While constraining global volumetric removal $\text{MRR}_t = \Delta V_t / \tau_t \le \text{MRR}_{\max}$ prevents gross spindle overload on 3D roughing passes (Eq.~\eqref{eq:total_loss}), evaluating trajectories under realistic metal-cutting physics reveals a subtle limitation of MRR-only surrogates: during light radial skimming or corner rest-milling ($a_e \ll D/2$), radial chip thinning drastically reduces the actual undeformed chip thickness $h_{\text{ex}}$ cut by each flute~\cite{martellotti1941milling}.

To optimize directly for physical chip load and minimal cycle time, we formulate \texttt{SubsegmentedCWEModel} (\texttt{algorithms.chipload\_opt}) coupled to our clean-room \texttt{cam.feeds\_and\_speeds} solver:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Material- and Tool-Specific Reference Physics:} Given stock alloy properties (Brinell hardness, specific cutting energy $k_p$, carbide surface speed $\text{SFM}_0$, base chip load $f_{z,0}$, slotting factor) and cutter geometry ($D$, flute count $z_f$, helix angle $\beta_h$, flute length, stickout $L_{\text{stick}}$, substrate Young's modulus $E$), our clean-room solver computes spindle speed $N_{\text{RPM}}$, baseline feed $f_{\text{base}}$, and target undeformed chip thickness $h_{\text{opt}}$ while enforcing six simultaneous physical caps: machine $f_{\max}$, spindle power $P_k \le P_{\max}$, machine and tool-core yield torque, cutting force $F_{c,k} \le F_{\max}$, and cantilever endmill deflection $\delta_k = \frac{F_{c,k} L_{\text{stick}}^3}{3 E I} \le \delta_{\max}$.
  \item \textbf{Chronological Sub-Segmented CWE and Radial Chip Thinning:} Long cutting moves are subdivided to $\le 2.0\text{ mm}$ stations ($k = 1,\dots,M$). Sweeping each station chronologically over the $0.25\text{ mm}$ 2.5D dexel heightfield $z_{\text{stock}}$ yields exact removed volume $\Delta V_k$, axial depth $a_{p,k}$, and effective radial immersion $u_k = \operatorname{clip}(a_{e,k}/D, 0, 1)$. By Martellotti's kinematics~\cite{martellotti1941milling}, the radial chip-thinning ratio $\psi(u_k)$ and actual undeformed chip thickness $h_{\text{ex},k}$ at tooth-passing frequency $f_{\text{tooth}} = (N_{\text{RPM}}/60)\,z_f$ are
  \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
      \psi(u_k) &= \begin{cases}
        \sqrt{4 u_k (1 - u_k)}, & 0 < u_k < 0.5, \\
        1, & u_k \ge 0.5,
      \end{cases} \\
      h_{\text{ex},k} &= \frac{f_k}{f_{\text{tooth}}}\,\psi(u_k).
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:chipload}
  \end{equation}
  In the forward pass, $\Delta V_k, a_{p,k}, u_k$ use exact hard dexel state; in the backward pass, STE analytical spatial sensitivities $\partial \Delta V_k / \partial \mathbf{p}$ propagate lateral engagement gradients so waypoints shift smoothly to equalize $u_k$ while per-station feeds $f_k$ track $h_{\text{ex},k} \to h_{\text{opt}}$ on engaged cuts and sprint at $f_{\max}$ across zero-engagement air segments.
  \item \textbf{Stock-Verified Empty-Air Detour Shortcutting:} After continuous STE-Adam convergence, contiguous runs of horizontal \texttt{G1} stations verified against $z_{\text{stock}}$ to remove $\Delta V_k = 0$ are collapsed into collision-checked \texttt{G0} clearance-plane bridges or straight \texttt{G1} chords, saving cycle time even when cutting feedrates are locked identically across methods.
\end{enumerate}

For 3D SDF benchmarking (\textbf{Exp1--Exp4}), the composite loss combines normalized cycle time, target gouge/residual, volumetric Peak MRR overload $\mathcal{B}_{\text{MRR}} = w_{\text{MRR}} \sum_{t=1}^T \max(0, \text{MRR}_t / \text{MRR}_{\max} - 1)^2$, exact 2D wall-clearance $\mathcal{B}_{\text{wall}}$, cylindrical holder-collision $\mathcal{B}_{\text{holder}}$, and rapid-stock $\mathcal{B}_{\text{rapid}}$ barriers:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{3D}} =\;& w_T \frac{T_{\text{cycle}}}{T_{\text{nom}}} + w_g \bar{V}_{\text{gouge}}^2 + w_r \bar{V}_{\text{res}}^2 \\
    &+ \mathcal{B}_{\text{MRR}} + \mathcal{B}_{\text{wall}} + \mathcal{B}_{\text{holder}} + \mathcal{B}_{\text{rapid}},
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}
while CWE chipload co-optimization (\textbf{Exp5--Exp6}) minimizes $\mathcal{L}_{\text{CWE}} = \frac{T_{\text{cycle}}}{T_{\text{nom}}} + w_h \sum_{k \in \mathcal{E}} \frac{\tau_k}{T_{\text{nom}}}\big(\frac{h_{\text{ex},k} - h_{\text{eff},k}}{h_{\text{opt}}}\big)^2 + \mathcal{B}_{\text{MRR}} + \mathcal{B}_{\text{res}} + \mathcal{B}_{\text{smooth}}$.

\section{Per-Part Optimization, Generalized RL, and Fast Inference-Time Overfitting}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Drift-Free Waypoint and Column-Group Co-Parameterizations}
Perturbing relative displacements $\Delta\mathbf{p}_t$ induces $O(T)$ cumulative random-walk drift across $37$--$370$ segments, driving downstream finishing passes into blueprint walls. We enforce zero-gouge kinematic structure by holding layer heights $\bar{p}_i^z$ fixed and bounding horizontal excursions by exact analytical Euclidean wall clearance $d_{\text{wall}}(\bar{\mathbf{p}}_i)$:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
    \mathbf{p}_i^{xy}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) &= \bar{\mathbf{p}}_i^{xy} + r_i \tanh(\mathbf{u}_{xy,i}), \\
    r_i &= \operatorname{clip}\!\big(d_{\text{wall}}(\bar{\mathbf{p}}_i) - \delta_{\text{pin}},\, 0,\, r_{\max}\big).
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:joint_spline}
\end{equation}
In our 3D spline model (\texttt{JointPathFeedSpline}), $\mathbf{u}_{xy} = \mathbf{B}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{pos}}$ uses a clamped cubic B-spline basis ($K_p \le 40$, $r_{\max} = 1.25\text{ mm}$) with plunge/retract pinning; in our sub-segmented CWE model (\texttt{SubsegmentedCWEModel}), interior waypoints sharing vertical plunges/retracts are coupled into $G$ vertical-column groups ($r_{\max} = 3.0\text{ mm}$) so vertical plunges remain strictly vertical while open-air lead-ins retract freely, paired with bounded sigmoid cutting feeds $f_k \in [f_{\min}, 0.995\,v_{\max}]$.

\subsection{Generalized Residual RL vs.\ Fast Inference-Time Trajectory Overfitting}
\label{sec:overfit_method}
A central architectural question for learning-based manufacturing is whether DiffCAM trains a \emph{generalized multi-part policy} or optimizes \emph{directly on each target part}—and how the two paradigms interact at deployment:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Direct Per-Part Differentiable Optimization (\texttt{DiffCAM STE-Adam} / \texttt{DiffCAM-Chipload}):} No neural network weights are trained across parts; instead, given any new part's initialized trajectory $\bar{\boldsymbol{\tau}}$, STE-Adam optimizes $\boldsymbol{\theta} \in \mathbb{R}^D$ ($D = 2G + M \approx 250\text{--}650$) directly against the differentiable simulator on that part ($1\text{--}3\text{ s}$ CPU/GPU).
  \item \textbf{Amortized Generalized RL Policies (\texttt{DiffPolicy-SHAC} / \texttt{Hard-PPO}):} A compact $\tanh$ MLP $\boldsymbol{\pi}_\psi(\mathbf{a}_t \mid \mathbf{s}_t)$ ($18$-D global SDF state or $12$-D local CWE state) is trained \emph{offline} across development parts using either first-order analytical simulator gradients (\texttt{SHAC}~\cite{xu2022accelerated}) or zero-order clipped policy gradients with GAE (\texttt{PPO}~\cite{schulman2017proximal,schulman2016gae}). On an unseen test part $\mathcal{P}_{\text{test}}$, a single forward pass produces $\boldsymbol{\theta}_0 = \boldsymbol{\pi}_{\psi^\star}(\mathbf{S}(\mathcal{P}_{\text{test}}))$ in $<\!2.5\text{ ms}$, but suffers finite-capacity approximation error across varying pocket/channel geometries.
  \item \textbf{Hybrid Warm-Start Inference-Time Trajectory Overfitting (\texttt{SHAC + DiffCAM-Overfit-$K$}):} Because our simulator is analytically differentiable at test time, we do not need to stop at $\boldsymbol{\theta}_0$ nor fine-tune $\psi$ via noisy policy gradients. Instead, we initialize $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(0)} \leftarrow \boldsymbol{\pi}_{\psi^\star}(\mathbf{S}(\mathcal{P}_{\text{test}}))$, detach the MLP bottleneck, and apply $K \in \{4, 8, 16, 32\}$ first-order CWE STE-Adam updates directly in trajectory space $\mathbb{R}^D$, followed by stock-verified air-detour shortcutting. Whereas zero-order RL fine-tuning on $\mathcal{P}_{\text{test}}$ faces gradient-estimator variance scaling as $\mathcal{O}(D / (\sigma^2 N_{\text{pop}}))$—rendering $16\text{--}160$ rollouts ineffective—exact per-station partials $\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{CWE}} / \partial f_k$ and $\partial \Delta V_k / \partial \mathbf{p}_g$ allow $K = 8\text{--}16$ steps ($200\text{--}450\text{ ms}$) to rapidly overfit to the exact unseen geometry and restore $>96\%$ optimal chipload compliance.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experimental Setup, Split Protocol, and Metrics}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Benchmark Suites, Clean-Room Catalogs, and Simulators}
\textbf{Single-Tool 16-Task Suite (\textbf{Exp1--Exp5b}):} Covers four clean-room 2.5D CAM strategy engines—\texttt{facing\_open\_boss} ($37$--$39$ segs), \texttt{pocket\_island} ($92$--$169$ segs), \texttt{adaptive\_channel} ($261$--$370$ segs), and \texttt{contour\_profile} ($156$--$188$ segs)—with 2 \texttt{dev} and 2 held-out \texttt{test} geometries per family ($16$ tasks, Al 6061-T6 billets $30\text{--}38 \times 24\text{--}32 \times 8\text{ mm}$, $D = 5.0\text{ mm}$ 3-flute coated-carbide flat endmill at $8000\text{ RPM}$, $v_{\max} = 20\text{ mm/s}$, $v_{\text{rapid}} = 100\text{ mm/s}$, $a_{\max} = 150\text{ mm/s}^2$).
\textbf{Multi-Tool \texttt{.stl} TAMP Suite (\textbf{Exp6}):} Four multi-feature 3D parts supplied as raw binary \texttt{.stl} solids across three alloys—\texttt{tamp-flanged-bracket} (Al 6061-T6), \texttt{tamp-sensor-housing} (Al 6061-T6, central island), \texttt{tamp-manifold-plate} (Al 7075-T6, $r_{\min}=1.5\text{ mm}$ tight corners + pocket-floor holes), and \texttt{tamp-stepped-mount} (Mild Steel 1018)—combining top facing, exterior steps, pockets, islands, spot chamfering, and \texttt{G83} peck drilling.

\subsection{Role of \texttt{dev} vs.\ Held-Out \texttt{test} Splits}
To ensure strict methodological clarity across direct trajectory optimization and policy learning:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Direct Per-Part Trajectory Optimization (\texttt{STE-Continuation-Adam}, \texttt{Annealed-Adam}, \texttt{DiffCAM-Chipload}, \texttt{Hard-CEM/CMA-ES}):} Uses the 8 \texttt{dev} tasks \emph{solely} to select and freeze solver hyperparameters (learning rates, barrier weights, $k$-schedule). On the 8 held-out \texttt{test} tasks, each optimizer starts cold from that test part's clean-room CAM seed without seeing any test geometry during tuning.
  \item \textbf{Generalized RL Policies (\texttt{DiffPolicy-SHAC}, \texttt{Hard-PPO}, \texttt{SHAC/PPO-Chipload}):} Neural policy weights $\psi^\star$ are trained \emph{strictly offline} on the \texttt{dev} split. Held-out \texttt{test} tasks evaluate true out-of-distribution geometric generalization, either \emph{zero-shot} ($K=0$ simulator backward passes on test) or via controlled \emph{inference-time trajectory overfitting} ($K > 0$ steps on test, \textbf{Exp3} \& \textbf{Exp5b}).
  \item \textbf{Automated Multi-Tool TAMP (\textbf{Exp6}):} Requires \emph{zero} training split; given an unseen \texttt{.stl} mesh and alloy, feature recognition, tool-library selection, and persistent-IPW optimization execute directly on the input part.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Physical Motivation and Definitions of Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:metrics_defs}
Every reported metric corresponds to a specific shop-floor productivity, surface-integrity, or machine-safety criterion:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Blended Cycle Time $T_{\text{cycle}}$ (s) and Gain $-\Delta T$ ($\%$):} Total program execution time under acceleration-bounded corner blending (Eq.~\eqref{eq:cycle_time}), decomposed into \textbf{Engaged \texttt{G1} Time} ($\Delta V_k > 0$, productive metal cutting), \textbf{Air-Cut \texttt{G1} Time} ($\Delta V_k = 0$, unproductive feed-rate traversal through previously cleared space), and \textbf{Rapid \texttt{G0} / ATC Time} (non-cutting repositioning and $6.0\text{ s}$ tool swaps).
  \item \textbf{Fair Same-Speed Time $T_{\text{same}}$ vs.\ Optimal-Chipload Time $T_{\text{opt}}$ (s):} Because naive nominal CAM uses a conservative constant feedrate, cycle-time gains can conflate faster speeds with shorter paths. $T_{\text{same}}$ locks \emph{all} methods' cutting moves to the exact same \texttt{feeds\_and\_speeds} baseline feedrate $f_{\text{base}}$ ($11.7\text{ mm/s}$ for $\varnothing 5\text{ mm}$ in 6061-T6), isolating pure geometric smoothing and air-shortcut savings; $T_{\text{opt}}$ evaluates full engagement-adaptive feed scheduling.
  \item \textbf{Target Gouge $V_{\text{gouge}}$ and Uncut Residual $V_{\text{res}}$ ($\text{mm}^3$):} Volume erroneously removed inside the finished CAD part ($V_{\text{gouge}}$, where any value above tolerance scraps the part) and excess stock left unremoved inside the operation target ($V_{\text{res}}$, which stalls downstream finishing tools), audited at $0.25\text{ mm}$ voxel / $0.125\text{ mm}$ step resolution alongside binary holder, rapid-stock, and vise-jaw collision screens.
  \item \textbf{Peak MRR ($\text{mm}^3/\text{s}$) and Overload ($\%$):} Maximum instantaneous volumetric removal rate $\max_t (\Delta V_t / \tau_t)$ and percentage excess above rated $\text{MRR}_{\max}$; exceeding $\text{MRR}_{\max}$ packs flutes with chips and stalls the spindle.
  \item \textbf{Actual Undeformed Chip Thickness $h_{\text{ex}}$ (mm), RMSE, and Optimal Chipload Band ($\%$):} Instantaneous radial-thinning-corrected chip thickness (Eq.~\eqref{eq:chipload}), its time-weighted root-mean-square error against target $h_{\text{opt}}$ ($0.0291\text{ mm/tooth}$ for $\varnothing 5\text{ mm}$ carbide in 6061-T6), and the percentage of engaged cutting time spent inside the healthy $[0.56\,h_{\text{opt}},\, 1.26\,h_{\text{opt}}]$ band (avoiding both rubbing work-hardening below $h_{\min}$ and edge fracture above $h_{\max}$).
  \item \textbf{Surrogate Conditioning \& Watertight STL $\Delta V$ ($\%$):} Analytical gradient signal-to-noise ratio ($\text{SNR} = \|\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{g}]\|_2 / \sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|\mathbf{g} - \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{g}]\|_2^2}$), cosine similarity to central hard finite differences, soft-to-hard $L_1$ occupancy drift, and enclosed volume discrepancy $\Delta V$ of closed manifold \texttt{.stl} meshes reconstructed independently by \texttt{CAMotics v1.2.0}.
\end{itemize}

Table~\ref{tab:exp5_overfit} (top) reveals three critical physical insights:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Why 3D MRR Alone Fails Under Radial Chip Thinning:} When re-evaluated under Martellotti chip-thinning mechanics, 3D \texttt{STE-Adam (MRR)} drops to $41.1\%$ optimal-band compliance ($h_{\text{ex}}$ RMSE $0.0163\text{ mm}$, $82.12\text{ s}$) because capping volumetric MRR without accounting for $\psi(a_e/D) < 1$ under-feeds light radial passes into the rubbing regime.
  \item \textbf{Pure Geometric Gain at Fair Matched Speed:} Locking every cutting segment to the \emph{exact same} feedrate $f_{\text{base}}$ across methods, \texttt{DiffCAM-Chipload} still reduces mean cycle time from $55.21\text{ s} \to \mathbf{53.60\text{ s}}$ ($\mathbf{-2.8\%}$ mean across all 8 parts, and up to $\mathbf{-7.9\%}$ on trochoidal \texttt{adapt-test-01}) purely by smoothing corner junctions, shifting waypoints up to $2.98\text{ mm}$ within safe wall clearance, and collapsing stock-verified empty-air \texttt{G1} detours into safe \texttt{G0} bridges.
  \item \textbf{Joint Optimal-Chipload Advantage:} Enabling per-station chipload optimization drives \texttt{DiffCAM-Chipload} to $\mathbf{48.07\text{ s}}$ ($\mathbf{-14.9\%}$ over physics-calibrated CAM, $100\%$ posted Haas pass) while keeping $\mathbf{97.7\%}$ of engaged cutting inside the optimal undeformed chip-thickness band ($h_{\text{ex}}$ RMSE $\mathbf{0.0053\text{ mm}}$), compared to $42.7\%$ / $42.6\%$ optimal-band compliance for zero-shot \texttt{SHAC-Chipload} and \texttt{PPO-Chipload}.
\end{enumerate}

In \textbf{Exp5b} (Table~\ref{tab:exp5_overfit} bottom, Fig.~\ref{fig:exp5}b), we test fast inference-time overfitting on each unseen \texttt{test} part (latencies include one-time $\sim\!0.32\text{ s}$ CWE dexel initialization for $K>0$). Zero-shot \texttt{SHAC} executes its neural forward pass in $\mathbf{3.5\text{ ms}}$, delivering instant speedups on canonical bosses/contours (e.g., $41.87\text{ s} \to 29.74\text{ s}$ on \texttt{face-test-00}) for an $+\mathbf{8.3\%}$ mean gain, but degrades on unseen long-horizon trochoidal channels (\texttt{adapt-test-01}) and achieves only $42.4\%$ optimal $h_{\text{ex}}$ compliance. Attempting to overfit \texttt{PPO} to the test trajectory at inference time via $16$--$160$ simulator rollouts (\texttt{PPO-Overfit}) consumes up to $3.52\text{ s}$ while failing to improve ($+8.3\% \to +7.1\%$, $41.0\%$ optimal $h_{\text{ex}}$), confirming that high-dimensional ($D \approx 250\text{--}650$) zero-order RL cannot practically adapt per part at planning time. Conversely, warm-starting from \texttt{SHAC-ZeroShot} and running $K = 8$--$32$ analytical CWE STE-Adam updates directly on $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ (\texttt{SHAC+Overfit}) executes in $\mathbf{0.48}$--$\mathbf{0.93\text{ s}}$, repairs out-of-distribution channel trajectories ($55.10\text{ s} \to \mathbf{50.26\text{ s}}$, $+\mathbf{11.0\%}$, cutting cross-part gain SD from $23.5\%$ to $9.4\%$), and lifts optimal-chipload compliance from $42.4\%$ to $\mathbf{95.6\%}$--$\mathbf{97.0\%}$. Even more strikingly, starting directly from the unbiased clean-room CAM seed (\texttt{Cold-DiffCAM}) reaches $\mathbf{50.16\text{ s}}$ ($+\mathbf{11.4\%}$, $\mathbf{98.2\%}$ optimal $h_{\text{ex}}$) in just $K=8$ steps ($\mathbf{0.59\text{ s}}$) and $\mathbf{49.58\text{ s}}$ ($+\mathbf{12.2\%}$) at $K=32$ ($\mathbf{1.04\text{ s}}$), converging to $\mathbf{48.07\text{ s}}$ ($+\mathbf{14.9\%}$) at $K=90$ ($2.12\text{ s}$).

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\textbf{Limitations and Path to Universal Zero-Effort Machining.}
While DiffCAM demonstrates end-to-end \texttt{.stl}-to-multi-tool G-code automation on single-setup prismatic parts with pockets, islands, exterior steps, and holes, four open challenges separate our prototype from universal ``slicer-like'' 3-axis automation: (1) \emph{Multi-setup 6-sided flipping and custom soft-jaw synthesis} for parts with undercuts or bottom-side features; (2) \emph{Simultaneous 3-axis ball-endmill sculptured surfacing} with continuous axial $z$ optimization; (3) \emph{Closed-loop frequency-domain chatter and thin-wall deflection modeling}~\cite{altintas2012manufacturing}; and (4) \emph{Physical Haas Mini Mill NGC cutting trials} measuring spindle load, cycle time, and surface roughness $R_a$.

\textbf{Conclusion.}
We challenged the manual paradigm of 3-axis CNC programming with \textbf{DiffCAM}, unifying automated task-level setup and clean-room multi-tool selection with classical CAM trajectory initialization, smooth-to-hard differentiable cutting simulation, and learning-based co-optimization. Across 6 experiments, DiffCAM delivers $42\%$ mean single-tool speedups, $2.0\times$ RL sample-efficiency gains, $97.7\%$ optimal chipload compliance, millisecond warm-start trajectory overfitting, and automated \texttt{.stl}-to-Haas-NGC multi-tool programs verified in 6-dialect and \texttt{CAMotics} simulation.
