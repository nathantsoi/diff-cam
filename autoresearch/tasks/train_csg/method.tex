\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We introduce \textbf{GradMill}, a differentiable-simulation approach to CNC
toolpath generation. Rather than learning a control policy, GradMill optimizes a
discrete tool trajectory directly: a sequence of per-step cutter displacements
is treated as the optimization variable, and gradients of a terminal geometry
loss are backpropagated through a differentiable constructive-solid-geometry
(CSG) carving simulator into the trajectory. The system is implemented in Taichi
with \texttt{ti.ad.Tape} autodiff.

A central finding of this work is that the simulator's gradients optimize a
\emph{differentiable surrogate} whose optimum is misaligned with the
\emph{deployable} (boolean) machining outcome: the soft union over-erodes, so
minimizing the soft loss systematically gouges the part under the exact boolean
carve. Unlike prior work that sidesteps this by encoding the answer in a
shape-specific initialization, our method is \textbf{shape-blind}: neither the
optimizer, the initialization, nor the loss inspects or branches on the target
shape name. The trajectory is initialized randomly and refined purely by
gradient descent. The soft/hard misalignment is instead closed by three
shape-blind mechanisms that act on the \emph{surrogate itself}: (i) smoothness
annealing (\texttt{k-anneal}) that aligns the training gradient with the
sharp deployable metric, (ii) a de-biasing constant (\texttt{loss-shift}) that
compensates the over-erosion so the soft loss targets the hard carve, and
(iii) a tool-length / reachability correction that lets the optimizer discover
the full part envelope. We describe each component below.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

The machine work volume contains a rectilinear stock block (a normalized
$[0,1]^3$ cube, default $1~\mathrm{in}^3$ at $0.5~\mathrm{mm}$ voxels) and a
target part $S_{\mathrm{tgt}}$ defined as the negative interior of an analytic
signed distance field (SDF) $\phi_{\mathrm{tgt}}(\mathbf{p})$. The target SDF may
be a primitive (sphere, cylinder, box, square pyramid) or a \emph{combined CSG}
field: a through-hole sphere $S = S_{\mathrm{sph}} \cap \neg S_{\mathrm{cyl}}$, or
a concave bowl $S = S_{\mathrm{sph}} \cap \neg (S_{\mathrm{sub}} \cap
\{z \le z_c\})$. A cylindrical end mill of radius $r$ and flute height $h$,
surmounted coaxially by a wider holder (the collet/spindle), removes material by
sweeping through the stock. A trajectory of $T$ tool positions
$\{\mathbf{x}_0, \dots, \mathbf{x}_{T-1}\}$ produces a carved stock
$S_{\mathrm{carved}} \subseteq S_{\mathrm{stock}}$. The objective is a trajectory
whose carved geometry matches the target, scored by the Dice coefficient between
the remaining material $S_{\mathrm{carved}}$ and the part $S_{\mathrm{tgt}}$.

\paragraph{Shape-blindness constraint.}
The target shape is exposed to the simulator \emph{only} as the pointwise SDF
$\phi_{\mathrm{tgt}}(\mathbf{p})$ used inside the loss. No component of the
method --- not the optimizer, the initialization, the loss, or any
hyperparameter schedule --- reads the shape label or branches on it. All
hyperparameters reported in Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments} are fixed across every
shape, including the combined-CSG targets. This is the operating constraint
under which the method is evaluated: it must generalize to arbitrary, unseen
shape combinations from geometry alone.

\subsection{Differentiable CSG Carving Simulator}
\label{sec:simulator}

\paragraph{Stock and target representation.}
The stock is a dense 3D scalar SDF field $\Phi_t(\mathbf{p})$ on a regular voxel
grid ($32^3$ default), where $\Phi<0$ denotes interior (remaining material). At
$t=0$ the stock is the full envelope: $\Phi_0 = \phi_{\mathrm{box}}$. The target
is an analytic SDF $\phi_{\mathrm{tgt}}$ evaluated pointwise, so no target
discretization is needed for the loss. Distances are measured in voxel units,
yielding an isotropic, physically-faithful metric.

\paragraph{Tool signed distance.}
The cutter is a swept cylinder of radius $r$ and height $h$. For the $t$-th cut,
spanning segment $\mathbf{x}_t \!\to\! \mathbf{x}_{t+1}$, the tool SDF at a query
point $\mathbf{p}$ combines the in-plane distance to the swept capsule axis with
the axial extent:
\begin{equation}
  \phi_{\mathrm{tool}}(\mathbf{p}, t) = \mathrm{smooth\_union}\!\left(d_{xy}(\mathbf{p}, t),\ d_z(\mathbf{p}, t);\, k\right),
\end{equation}
where $d_{xy}$ is the point-to-segment distance in $xy$ minus $r$, $d_z$ measures
the axial band centered at $z_{\mathrm{base}}(t) + h/2$ with half-extent $h/2$,
and the tool's base $z$ is interpolated along the segment. The smooth union uses
the log-sum-exp smooth maximum
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{smooth\_max}(a,b;k) = \tfrac{1}{k}\log\!\left(e^{ka} + e^{kb}\right),
\end{equation}
with sharpness $k$. A separate holder SDF, mirroring this geometry with the
holder radius and a $z$-range beginning at the tool top, models the spindle
above the flutes.

\paragraph{Differentiable carving.}
Each cut updates the stock by a smooth boolean union, removing the tool's
interior from the remaining material:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:soft-carve}
  \Phi_{t+1}(\mathbf{p}) = \mathrm{smooth\_max}\!\left(\Phi_t(\mathbf{p}),\ -\phi_{\mathrm{tool}}(\mathbf{p}, t);\, k\right).
\end{equation}
The whole unrolled loop --- position reconstruction, carving, and loss --- runs
inside a single \texttt{ti.ad.Tape}, so $\partial \mathcal{L}/\partial \mathbf{x}_t$
flows back through every cut. Because each step's smooth maximum adds a bias of
$\mathcal{O}(\log 2 / k)$ per union, the differentiable carve
\emph{over-erodes} relative to the exact boolean; this is the surrogate
misalignment our method targets (Sec.~\ref{sec:gap}).

\paragraph{Speed-limit constraint.}
Real machines clamp feed and traverse rates. We model this with a differentiable
per-step clip: the commanded displacement $\boldsymbol{\delta}_t$ implies a speed
$|\boldsymbol{\delta}_t \cdot \mathbf{L}|/\Delta t$ (mm/s, with $\mathbf{L}$ the
per-axis envelope). If it exceeds the regime cap, $\boldsymbol{\delta}_t$ is
scaled down to run exactly at the cap (direction preserved). The regime is
\emph{feed} (cutting speed, when the destination probes within a safe distance of
remaining stock) or \emph{rapid} (traverse, when clear). This is a hard gate with
zero gradient through the regime selection itself, while gradients still flow
through the clipped magnitude into $\boldsymbol{\delta}_t$.

\subsection{Trajectory Parametrization and Initialization}
\label{sec:param}

The trajectory is parametrized by $T{-}1$ per-step displacements
$\{\boldsymbol{\delta}_t\}_{t=0}^{T-2}$, with the first tool position fixed at a
canonical start $\mathbf{x}_0 = (0.5, 0.5, 1.0)$ (above the stock center).
Positions are the cumulative clipped sum
$\mathbf{x}_{t+1} = \mathbf{x}_t + \mathrm{clip}(\boldsymbol{\delta}_t)$.
The parameters are stored as a PyTorch tensor with
\texttt{requires\_grad=True} and optimized by Adam.

\paragraph{Initialization modes.}
The framework provides a family of initialization modes for the displacement
parameters, which we describe here because the choice is consequential to the
shape-blindness claim. The modes split into three classes:

\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
  \item \textbf{Random} (\texttt{init\_mode=random}, the default): the
        displacements are drawn i.i.d.\ uniformly,
        $\boldsymbol{\delta}_t \sim \mathcal{U}[-\sigma_{\mathrm{init}},\, \sigma_{\mathrm{init}}]^3$
        with $\sigma_{\mathrm{init}}{=}0.05$, around the canonical start. No
        geometric prior about the target is injected.
  \item \textbf{Structured raster/spiral} (\texttt{raster}, \texttt{raster\_fine},
        \texttt{raster\_fine\_wide}, \texttt{spiral}): analytic toolpaths --- a
        boustrophedon (zigzag) sweep over the cross-section at descending
        $z$-levels, or a descending spiral. These inject a machining prior (the
        tool pre-covers the part envelope) but are \emph{geometry-agnostic}: the
        path shape is fixed and does not read the target. The coarse variants
        (\texttt{raster}, \texttt{spiral}) fail in practice because their
        per-step displacements exceed the feed speed cap and are magnitude-clipped
        by the constraint of Sec.~\ref{sec:simulator}, destroying the intended
        path; the \texttt{raster\_fine} variants are constructed so every step is
        within the cap.
  \item \textbf{Surface-following} (\texttt{shell}, \texttt{zlayer}): a
        descending helix / $z$-level finishing orbit whose radius at each $z$
        tracks the target's cross-section, so the cutter orbits just outside the
        part surface and sweeps the waste annulus without gouging. Crucially, the
        surface is read \emph{shape-agnostically} from the baked target SDF grid:
        the per-$z$ cross-section radius is computed as the maximum in-plane
        distance from the stock center among voxels with
        $\phi_{\mathrm{tgt}} < 0$ at that slice, with no reference to any shape
        name or shape parameter. This generalizes to any target, including
        combined-CSG and unseen shapes.
\end{enumerate}

All three classes are shape-blind under our definition: none reads the shape
label. They differ in \emph{how much} geometric prior they inject, from none
(random) to a full surface-following path derived from the SDF field.

\paragraph{Operating-point choice: random.}
The final operating point uses the \texttt{random} initialization for the
primitive targets. This is the most conservative shape-blind choice --- it
injects no prior whatsoever --- and is selected because, once the surrogate
corrections of Sec.~\ref{sec:gap} are active, the initialization basin is no
longer decisive: with $k$-anneal enabled, a structured \texttt{raster\_fine}
init and a \texttt{random} init converge to comparable deployable Dice (e.g.\
sphere seed~1: $0.788$ vs $0.784$ at $k_{\mathrm{final}}{=}80$; the gap is within
seed noise and $k$-anneal dominates). Placing the burden of geometry discovery
on the (de-biased) gradient, rather than on the init, is what makes the method
truly shape-blind: the same configuration trains a sphere, a pyramid, or a
through-hole sphere with no modification. The surface-following inits remain
available as a shape-blind alternative when the random basin is insufficient
(e.g.\ for the concave-bowl combined-CSG target, where \texttt{raster\_fine}
lifts Dice by $\sim 0.03$ over random at the same budget), but they are not
required for the primitives.

\subsection{Loss Function}
\label{sec:loss}

The terminal geometry loss is computed on the final stock $\Phi_T$ against the
target. SDFs are converted to occupancy via a sigmoid $\sigma(\cdot)$ with a
1-voxel scale, giving smooth, differentiable occupancy fields
$o_{\mathrm{stock}} = \sigma(\Phi_T)$ and $o_{\mathrm{tgt}} = \sigma(\phi_{\mathrm{tgt}})$.
Two error terms are defined at each voxel:
\begin{align}
  \mathrm{gouge}    &= o_{\mathrm{tgt}}\,(1 - o_{\mathrm{stock}})  \quad \text{(part material removed)}, \\
  \mathrm{residual} &= (1 - o_{\mathrm{tgt}})\,o_{\mathrm{stock}}  \quad \text{(waste material left)},
\end{align}
and the objective is their weighted sum of squares,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:loss}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{geom}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{p}} \left[\, w_{\mathrm{gouge}}\,\mathrm{gouge}^2 + w_{\mathrm{residual}}\,\mathrm{residual}^2 \,\right].
\end{equation}
The residual term rewards removing waste; the gouge term penalizes cutting into
the part. Optional differentiable barriers are added to $\mathcal{L}$: a one-sided
holder-penetration barrier $\mathrm{relu}(\mathrm{margin} - \phi_{\mathrm{holder}})^2$
that is zero with zero gradient whenever the holder has clearance, and several
trajectory regularizers (path-length, jerk, air-cut, contour-hug). These
regularizers are inactive in the final operating point: they trade off the
geometry objective for marginal air-cut reduction without improving the
deployable metric (Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}).

\subsection{The Optimization and Evaluation Loop}
\label{sec:optim}

The training and evaluation lifecycle of \texttt{train\_csg} is structured as an
interleaved regime of differentiable surrogate optimization, periodic exact
geometric verification, and post-processing safety enforcement
(Fig.~\ref{fig:optim_eval_loop}). The optimization loop pauses soft refinement
every $K$ iterations to execute an exact boolean carve from the canonical tool
start, scoring deployable metrics (Dice, ASD, HD95) used for best-checkpoint
selection.

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{optimization_evaluation_loop.png}
  \caption{\textbf{The \texttt{train\_csg} differentiable optimization, evaluation, and early-stopping loop.} At each training iteration $i$, commanded tool displacements $\boldsymbol{\delta}^{(i)}$ are passed through speed-limit and $z$-floor subgradient gates into the differentiable CSG carving simulator unrolled within a \texttt{ti.ad.Tape}. The smoothness $k^{(i)}$ is annealed (Sec.~\ref{sec:gap}) and the loss is de-biased by $\Delta$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:gap}). Backpropagated gradients $\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{geom}}/\partial\boldsymbol{\delta}$ are $\ell_2$-norm clipped to update the trajectory parameters via Adam. Periodically (every $K$ iterations), soft refinement branches to execute an exact boolean carve from the canonical tool start $\mathbf{x}_0$, scoring deployable metrics (Dice, ASD, HD95). Whenever deployable hard Dice surpasses the historical maximum, an early-stopping authority snapshots the exact pre-step trajectory and metric dictionary into memory.}
  \label{fig:optim_eval_loop}
\end{figure*}

\paragraph{Iterative differentiable forward pass.}
At each training iteration $i \in \{0, \dots, N_{\mathrm{iters}}-1\}$, the current PyTorch displacement parameters $\boldsymbol{\delta}^{(i)} \in \mathbb{R}^{(T-1) \times 3}$ are transferred to Taichi field tensors. The forward simulation unrolls inside a \texttt{ti.ad.Tape(loss=sim.loss)} context over $T$ discrete tool positions. Position reconstruction and carving are interleaved with two hard subgradient gating functions:
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
  \item \textbf{Kinematic speed clipping:} Commanded displacements $\boldsymbol{\delta}_t$ exceeding the kinematic regime limit (cutting feed rate $\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{feed}}$ when proximal to stock, or rapid traverse $\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{rapid}}$ when clear) are magnitude-scaled along their commanded vector to saturate exactly at the velocity ceiling.
  \item \textbf{Z-floor collision clamping:} To prevent catastrophic toolholder plunges into remaining workpiece material, the executed tool base altitude $z_{\mathrm{base}}(t)$ is bounded from below by a safety floor $z_{\mathrm{floor}} = z_{\mathrm{part\_bottom}} - \epsilon / L_z$ (default margin $\epsilon = 1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$).
\end{enumerate}
Both constraints act as exact subgradient gates: gradients flow unimpeded through active interior displacements, but drop to zero across clamped bounds. Each step then updates the soft stock scalar field via the log-sum-exp smooth boolean union (Eq.~\ref{eq:soft-carve}), accumulating into the terminal soft occupancy field $\Phi_T$.

\paragraph{Loss backpropagation and parameter updates.}
The terminal geometry objective $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{geom}}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:loss}) is evaluated over the soft occupancy fields. Backpropagation through the unrolled \texttt{ti.ad.Tape} computes the exact analytic gradient $\mathbf{g}^{(i)} = \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\delta}} \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{geom}}(\boldsymbol{\delta}^{(i)})$. Because CSG operations introduce sharp local curvature across workpiece boundaries, unconstrained gradient steps can induce numerical oscillation or NaN divergence when tool boundaries graze thin features. We mitigate this with an $\ell_2$-norm clipping operator across the full trajectory gradient:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{\mathbf{g}}^{(i)} = \begin{cases}
    \mathbf{g}^{(i)}, & \text{if } \|\mathbf{g}^{(i)}\|_2 \le c_{\mathrm{grad}}, \\
    c_{\mathrm{grad}} \frac{\mathbf{g}^{(i)}}{\|\mathbf{g}^{(i)}\|_2}, & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with threshold $c_{\mathrm{grad}} = 0.5$. Parameters are updated via Adam ($\beta_1=0.9, \beta_2=0.999$) at a constant learning rate of $\alpha = 1 \times 10^{-3}$. Aggressive rates ($\alpha \ge 5 \times 10^{-3}$) overshoot the narrow carving basin, causing transient Dice peaks that degrade into over-eroded solutions; conservative rates ($\alpha \le 5 \times 10^{-4}$) underfit within the compute budget.

\paragraph{Periodic exact evaluation and best-checkpoint snapshotting.}
Every $K$ iterations (default $K{=}10$) and at the final epoch, the loop pauses soft refinement to execute an exact verification pass: the tool origin is reset to the canonical $\mathbf{x}_0$ and the simulator executes an exact boolean carve (Eq.~\ref{eq:hard-carve}) using the sharp tool SDF, bypassing log-sum-exp over-erosion. The resulting hard remaining material $\Phi_T^{\mathrm{hard}}$ is evaluated against the target to compute deployable Dice, ASD, and HD95. Because soft loss minimization can transiently diverge from deployable Dice, the loop continuously monitors hard Dice; whenever it surpasses the historical maximum, an in-memory snapshot is taken of the pre-step trajectory and its co-measured metrics. The reported and deployed result is this best checkpoint; we do not re-evaluate restored parameters at the conclusion of training (the hard carve has small nondeterminism under GPU atomic-add reductions), so the reported number corresponds exactly to the saved trajectory.

\paragraph{Post-processing.}
Following training, the snapshotted best checkpoint enters a post-processing safety backstop (\texttt{trunc} stage). The trajectory is hard-carved step-by-step to query holder-to-stock clearance; any segment with imminent toolholder collision is cleanly truncated a safety margin ($1.0\,\mathrm{mm}$) prior to contact. The collision-free path is exported to machine-ready NC G-code (\texttt{export}) and validated via round-trip execution and rendering (\texttt{eval}, \texttt{viz}).

\subsection{Closing the Soft/Hard Carve Gap}
\label{sec:gap}

The differentiable carve (Eq.~\ref{eq:soft-carve}) uses \texttt{smooth\_max}, so
its per-step over-erosion accumulates over $T$ cuts. The deployable machining
outcome is instead an \emph{exact} boolean union,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:hard-carve}
  \Phi_{t+1}^{\mathrm{hard}}(\mathbf{p}) = \max\!\left(\Phi_t^{\mathrm{hard}}(\mathbf{p}),\ -\phi_{\mathrm{tool}}^{\mathrm{sharp}}(\mathbf{p}, t)\right),
\end{equation}
using a sharp (non-smoothed) tool SDF. The training loss
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{geom}}$ is evaluated on $\Phi_T$ (soft), but the
\emph{deployable} metric --- the Dice coefficient reported and used for
checkpoint selection --- is evaluated on $\Phi_T^{\mathrm{hard}}$.

This creates a structural decoupling: the soft union over-erodes, so a trajectory
that minimizes $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{geom}}$ systematically over-removes material
relative to the target, and under the exact boolean carve that over-removal
gouges the part. With a fixed soft sharpness $k{=}10$ and a random initialization,
the optimizer is pulled toward the soft optimum and hard Dice stalls low
(e.g.\ sphere $\approx 0.64$, pyramid $\approx 0.43$ at $k{=}10$;
Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}). Rather than sidestep this by encoding the answer in
the initialization, our method closes the gap with three shape-blind mechanisms
that act on the surrogate so that \emph{following the gradient} becomes
deployable-metric-improving.

\paragraph{Mechanism 1: smoothness annealing (\texttt{k-anneal}).}
The over-erosion per union is $\mathcal{O}(\log 2 / k)$, so sharpening $k$
reduces the bias. However, training the entire run at high $k$ is unstable: the
log-sum-exp saturates and gradients vanish as $k \to \infty$, and a sharp
surrogate from the start offers no smooth basin for the random init to descend
into. We therefore linearly anneal the smoothness over training,
\begin{equation}
  k^{(i)} = k_{\mathrm{init}} + (k_{\mathrm{final}} - k_{\mathrm{init}})\,\frac{i}{N_{\mathrm{iters}}},
\end{equation}
from an exploratory $k_{\mathrm{init}}{=}10$ to a high-fidelity
$k_{\mathrm{final}}{=}150$. Early iterations optimize a smooth, well-conditioned
surrogate that the random init can descend; late iterations sharpen the
surrogate toward the exact boolean, so the terminal gradient direction aligns
with the deployable metric. This is purely a \emph{gradient-bias reduction} lever:
$k$ does not appear in the hard eval (Eq.~\ref{eq:hard-carve} is $k$-independent),
only in the training forward, so annealing $k$ reshapes the loss landscape
without changing the scored quantity. Empirically, $k_{\mathrm{final}}{=}150$ is
the sweet spot; higher values ($k_{\mathrm{final}} \in \{500, 1000\}$) both
degrade positive-feature Dice and fail to close the gap on narrow negative
features (Sec.~\ref{sec:experiments}).

\paragraph{Mechanism 2: de-biasing loss shift (\texttt{loss-shift}).}
Even at the terminal sharpness, a residual over-erosion remains: the soft
occupancy the loss sees is systematically more-carved than the hard outcome. We
compensate by adding a constant bias $\Delta$ to the stock SDF before the loss
occupancy,
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:loss-shift}
  o_{\mathrm{stock}} = \sigma\!\left(\Phi_T + \Delta\right), \qquad \Delta = -0.5~\text{vox}.
\end{equation}
Because $\Phi_T < 0$ inside remaining material, a negative $\Delta$ makes the
loss perceive \emph{more} material remaining than the soft carve produced, easing
further removal and counteracting the over-erosion so the soft loss optimum
aligns with the (less-eroded) hard deployable outcome. The principled magnitude
is the accumulated per-union bias evaluated at the final sharpness,
$\Delta \approx -\log 2 \cdot k_{\mathrm{ref}} / k_{\mathrm{final}} \approx -0.24$
vox (with $k_{\mathrm{ref}}{=}51$ the reference sharpness at which the bias is one
voxel); the empirical sweet spot $\Delta{=}-0.5$ is consistent in sign and order
of magnitude. The direction was verified empirically in both signs: positive
$\Delta$ monotonically \emph{increased} over-carve (carved voxels
$57{,}317 \to 70{,}332$ across $\Delta \in \{+0.24, +0.5, +1.0\}$) and lowered
Dice; negative $\Delta$ reduced over-carve and lifted both soft and hard Dice,
with $\Delta{=}-0.5$ optimal and $\Delta{=}-1.0$ over-correcting into
under-carve. The shift is applied identically to every shape.

\paragraph{Mechanism 3: tool-length / reachability correction.}
The deployable trajectory must be collision-free: the wider holder above the
flutes must clear the stock. With a short tool ($h{=}25~\mathrm{mm}$ on a
$1~\mathrm{in}$ stock), the holder collides whenever the tool base descends below
the stock top, so the $z$-floor gate truncates the trajectory to roughly the
first third of waypoints (e.g.\ $40/128$), and the optimizer can never reach the
lower part envelope --- a reachability ceiling that no loss shaping can overcome.
We set $h{=}50~\mathrm{mm}$, which clears the holder throughout the part envelope
on the $1~\mathrm{in}$ stock and yields a full-length deployable trajectory
(viz Dice $=$ train Dice, no truncation). Tool length saturates at this point:
$h{=}75~\mathrm{mm}$ matches $h{=}50~\mathrm{mm}$ within seed noise, a dead lever.
Tool length is set per \emph{stock size} (a physical reachability ratio), not per
shape, preserving shape-blindness; on a $2~\mathrm{in}$ stock we scale
proportionally to $h{=}100~\mathrm{mm}$.

\subsection{Summary}
\label{sec:method-summary}

GradMill optimizes a CNC trajectory by backpropagation through a differentiable
CSG simulator. The simulator's gradients are correctly computed and applied each
iteration, but they minimize a soft surrogate whose over-erosion bias makes it
misaligned with the deployable boolean outcome. The method closes this
soft/hard gap with three shape-blind mechanisms --- smoothness annealing, a
de-biasing loss shift, and a tool-length reachability correction --- so that
following the gradient from a random initialization improves the deployable
metric rather than degrading it. A best-checkpoint protocol preserves the
deployable optimum against transient surrogate divergence. The entire method is
shape-blind: the target enters only as a pointwise SDF in the loss, and every
hyperparameter is fixed across all shapes and combined-CSG targets.
